## Why Client-Side Matters

A discount calculation is about your money — the price of something you are thinking of buying, and how much you would save. That is your business, not a server's. This tool runs entirely inside your browser, so the prices you type never leave your device, are never stored, and disappear when you close the tab. There is no account, no tracking, and no reason a sale-price check should involve anyone else.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Enter the original price of the item, then type the discount percentage — or tap one of the preset buttons for common sale tiers: 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%, 50%, or 70%. If your area adds sales tax, you can enter a tax percentage too, and the tool applies it to the discounted price to show the final checkout cost. The result appears instantly: the final price, the amount you save, and a breakdown you can copy. Change the percentage or the price and everything updates at once, so comparing two different discounts takes seconds.

## Feature Deep Dive

The maths is simple but easy to get wrong in your head. The discounted price is the original price multiplied by one minus the discount divided by one hundred, so a 20% discount on a 50-unit item leaves 40. Tax is applied to the discounted subtotal, not the original price, which matches how shops normally charge it. The preset buttons cover the tiers retailers use most, so a quick tap answers the question without typing. You can pick a currency symbol — dollars, euros, pounds, yen, or rupees — so the result reads like a real price in your own money. The breakdown also shows the saving separately from the final price, which helps when you want to know both what you pay and what you kept.

## Use Cases

Shoppers check a sale price before reaching the till, working out whether a "30% off" tag is actually a good deal. Bargain hunters compare two shops offering different discounts on the same item and see which final price is lower. Small business owners and market sellers work out sale prices for their own stock, including tax, before printing a price tag. Students and anyone splitting a bill use it to apply a percentage off a shared total. It is also handy during big sale events, when a quick check stops an impulse buy that only looks cheap.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between "20% off" and "save 20"?**

"20% off" is a percentage of the price, so the saving grows with the price; "save 20" is a fixed amount in your currency. This tool works with percentages — for a fixed-amount saving, just subtract it from the price yourself.

**Can I use the calculator for a discount on a discounted price?**

Yes, in two steps. First calculate the price after the first discount, then enter that result as the original price and apply the second discount. That gives the correct stacked result, since percentages multiply rather than add.

**Does the calculator include tax in the saving?**

No. The saving shown is the discount amount before tax. Tax is calculated on the discounted price and added separately, so the final checkout total includes it but the saving figure stays clean.

**Why is the final price not a round number?**

Because percentages rarely divide neatly. The tool shows the exact result, including cents or pence, so you can see precisely what you will pay rather than a rounded guess.
