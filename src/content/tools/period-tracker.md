## Why Client-Side Matters

Cycle dates are deeply personal health information, and they deserve to stay on your own device. Many period apps send this data to a server or an account; this tool does not. The entire calculation runs inside your browser, so the dates you enter are never uploaded, never logged, and never stored anywhere. There is no account, no tracking, and nothing left behind when you close the tab — just the dates you asked for.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Pick the first day of your last period using the calendar picker. Then enter your average cycle length in days — the tool starts with 28, the most common length, but you should change it to your own average if you know it. Enter your typical period duration too; the default is 5 days. Click "Calculate Cycle Dates" and the tool shows your next predicted period date, your estimated ovulation date, and your fertile window, plus how many days away the next period is. Change any input and recalculate to see how the dates shift.

## Feature Deep Dive

The predictions are built from your own cycle history, not from a generic calendar. The next period is estimated by adding your average cycle length to the first day of your last period. Ovulation is estimated at around 14 days before the next period starts, which is the more reliable way to place it than counting forward from the last period. The fertile window covers the days around ovulation when conception is most likely. All three figures are estimates: real cycles vary from month to month, with stress, illness, travel, and age all able to shift the dates by several days.

## Use Cases

People planning a holiday, a trip, or an important event check whether their period is likely to fall on those dates. Those trying to conceive use the fertile window to time their efforts, and those avoiding pregnancy use the dates as one piece of awareness — not as a sole method. Anyone tracking their cycle for health reasons gets a quick prediction without handing their data to an app. People starting hormonal treatment or coming off it use the tracker to watch how their cycle settles into a new pattern. It is also simply useful for being prepared, with supplies ready before the predicted date arrives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How accurate are the predicted dates?**

They are estimates based on your average cycle, and most people find them accurate within a few days. If your cycle varies a lot from month to month, the predictions will be less precise — keep a note of your real start dates and use your true average.

**What should I do if my cycle is irregular?**

Use your average over the last three to six months rather than a single cycle, and treat the dates as a rough guide. If your cycles are very irregular or have changed suddenly, speak to a healthcare professional, as it can be worth checking.

**Can I use this tracker as contraception?**

No. Cycle predictions are not a reliable contraceptive method on their own, because ovulation can shift unexpectedly. If you want to avoid pregnancy, use a recognised contraceptive method and treat these dates as information only.

**Do I need to create an account or save my dates?**

No. Nothing is saved at all — the tool works from the dates you type in each visit. If you want a record, note your cycle dates privately in your own calendar or notebook.
