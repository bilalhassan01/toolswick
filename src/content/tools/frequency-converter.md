## Feature Deep Dive

Frequency is measured in cycles per second, and hertz is the base unit: one hertz is one cycle per second. The metric prefixes scale it up by thousands — kilohertz is 1,000 Hz, megahertz is 1,000,000 Hz, and gigahertz is 1,000,000,000 Hz — which is why radio stations, processors, and wireless signals each use a different prefix. Rotational speed uses revolutions per minute, and the tool converts it by dividing by 60 to get hertz. Angular velocity appears too, in radians per second and degrees per second, which is the form used in physics and robotics. The comparison table makes the relationships visible at a glance instead of hiding them behind a single answer. It is also a good way to build intuition: seeing 60 RPM next to 1 Hz, or 1 MHz next to 1,000 kHz, makes the scale of each prefix concrete in a way a single conversion does not. Because results update live, you can slide through values and watch every unit move together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between frequency and angular velocity?**

Frequency counts complete cycles per second, measured in hertz. Angular velocity measures how fast something rotates in radians per second, and one full cycle equals 2π radians — so the two are related but not the same unit.

**Why do radio stations use megahertz while audio uses hertz?**

It is the same unit, just a different scale. Radio waves cycle millions of times per second, so megahertz keeps the number readable, while audible sound cycles tens to thousands of times per second, so plain hertz is clearer.

**How many hertz is one kilohertz?**

Exactly 1,000 hertz. Each metric step multiplies by 1,000: 1 kHz is 1,000 Hz, 1 MHz is 1,000 kHz, and 1 GHz is 1,000 MHz.

**Can I convert terahertz?**

Yes. Terahertz is included in the table, at 1,000 gigahertz. It is used for very high frequencies, such as infrared light and some advanced wireless research.
