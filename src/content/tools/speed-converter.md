## Why Client-Side Matters

Speed conversions come up on road trips, in flight plans, on boats, and in physics homework, and they should be instant and private. This tool converts entirely inside your own browser, with no server involved and no data leaving your device. ## Step-by-Step Guide

Type the speed value into the input box. Then select the unit you are converting from, such as kilometres per hour, miles per hour, metres per second, knots, or Mach. Next, choose the unit you want to convert to, and the converted speed appears immediately. If you need the result elsewhere, click "Copy Result" to copy the formatted value to your clipboard. Change any number or switch either unit at any time, and the answer updates in real time.

## Feature Deep Dive

The converter covers the speed units people actually meet. Kilometres per hour and miles per hour are the road units, metres per second is the scientific standard and the base SI unit of velocity, knots are used in aviation and sailing, and Mach describes speed relative to the speed of sound. One knot is defined as one nautical mile per hour, which is exactly 1.852 kilometres per hour or about 1.151 miles per hour. Mach is calculated at standard sea-level conditions, around 15 degrees Celsius, where the speed of sound is about 340.29 metres per second, so a Mach number always means something concrete rather than a vague idea of fast. The copy button keeps the formatted result ready to paste into a message, a document, or a flight plan.

## Use Cases

Drivers use the converter when travelling abroad, translating speed limits and speedometer readings between miles per hour and kilometres per hour. Pilots and sailors work in knots, and convert to road units when explaining a speed to passengers or comparing with land travel. Runners and cyclists convert paces between minutes per kilometre and minutes per mile, and students use it for physics problems where the answer needs to be in metres per second. Anyone reading a weather report, a flight tracker, or a vehicle specification uses it to put an unfamiliar unit into one they understand at a glance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What speed is Mach 1?**

Mach 1 is the speed of sound, about 340.29 metres per second at standard sea-level conditions, which is roughly 1,225 kilometres per hour. It changes with temperature and altitude, so the tool calculates it at standard sea-level conditions of around 15 degrees Celsius.

**Why do boats and planes use knots instead of km/h?**

Because a knot is one nautical mile per hour, and nautical miles are tied to the Earth's geometry, with one minute of latitude equal to one nautical mile. That makes knots far more convenient for navigation charts, where distances are measured in nautical miles.

**Can I convert a speed in metres per second to miles per hour?**

Yes. Enter the value, choose metres per second as the starting unit and miles per hour as the target, and the result appears instantly. You can convert between any two of the supported units in either direction.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. Every conversion runs entirely in your browser. The numbers you enter are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
