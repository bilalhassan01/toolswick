## Why Client-Side Matters

Your grades are your own business. Working out a weighted average should not mean uploading your marks to a server or creating an account somewhere. This tool does every calculation inside your browser, so the scores and weights you type never leave your device, are never stored, and vanish when you close the tab. It is a private, instant answer to a question only you need to know.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Add each assessment — homework, quizzes, assignments, and exams — with the score you earned and the weight your syllabus gives that category. For example, homework might be worth 20% and the final exam 30%. Click "+ Add Assessment" to add as many components as your course has. The tool shows your current weighted average and the matching letter grade right away. There is also a final-exam planner: enter your target grade and the exam's weight, and it tells you the exact score you need on the final to reach it.

## Feature Deep Dive

A weighted grade is not a simple average: each category score is multiplied by its decimal weight, and the results are added together. That is why a poor exam score hurts more than a poor homework score when the exam carries more weight. If your weights do not add up to 100%, the calculator normalises them automatically, so the result still makes sense. The letter grade follows the usual scale from A+ down to F. The final-exam planner works the formula backwards: it takes your current average, your target, and the exam's weight, and solves for the score you need — which is the number students actually want the night before the exam. Because the planner uses the same normalised weights as the main calculation, its answer stays consistent with the average shown above it.

## Use Cases

Students check where they stand midway through a term, before the final exam decides everything. Anyone who did badly on one assignment sees how much it really matters, given its weight, instead of worrying blindly. Exam candidates use the planner to set a concrete target score rather than a vague "do well". Teachers and tutors demonstrate to a class how weighting works, using real syllabus numbers. Parents helping with study planning can see which category deserves the most revision time for the biggest gain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do if I do not know a category's weight yet?**

Check your course syllabus — the weights are usually listed there. If a weight is genuinely unknown, leave that category out for now and add it once you know; the calculator normalises whatever weights you have entered.

**My teacher drops the lowest homework score. Can the calculator handle that?**

Not automatically. Leave out the dropped assignment when you enter your scores, and the average will reflect the remaining work, which matches how your teacher calculates it.

**What if the score I need on the final is over 100%?**

Then the target is not reachable through the final alone, even with a perfect score. The planner will show this honestly — at that point the realistic move is to aim for the best possible grade and focus on the remaining coursework too.

**Does the calculator round the final score needed?**

The planner gives the exact score required. In practice, aim slightly above it — a small margin covers marking variation and keeps the target safely in reach.
