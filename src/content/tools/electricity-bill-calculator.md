## Why Client-Side Matters

An electricity estimate involves your home, your appliances, and your tariff — details of your household that do not need to leave your device. This tool runs the whole calculation inside your browser, so the wattage, hours, and rate you type are never uploaded, never stored, and vanish when you close the tab. You get a clear picture of what an appliance costs to run without an account or any data leaving your home.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Enter the appliance's power rating in watts, then how many hours a day it typically runs. Finally, enter your electricity rate per kilowatt-hour — the price your supplier charges, which you can find on your bill. The tool shows the estimated cost straight away, broken down by day, month, and year. You can pick a currency symbol so the figures read in your own money. Try different hours or a different rate and watch the totals update instantly, which makes it easy to compare appliances side by side.

## Feature Deep Dive

The core formula is simple: kilowatt-hours equal watts multiplied by hours of use, divided by one thousand. A 100-watt bulb running for ten hours uses one kilowatt-hour. Multiply that by your rate per kilowatt-hour and you have the cost. The day, month, and year breakdown is what makes the numbers meaningful — an appliance that costs a few cents a day can still add up to a noticeable amount over a year. Spotting the heavy users, like air conditioners, heaters, and old fridges, is the point: those are the devices where a small change in usage makes a real difference to the bill. Comparing two appliances side by side is just as useful: a newer efficient model often pays for its higher price through lower running costs within a year or two.

## Use Cases

Homeowners check how much an air conditioner or space heater actually costs to run before leaving it on all day. Renters compare the running costs of appliances when choosing between two models in a shop. Anyone trying to cut their bill finds the biggest energy users first instead of guessing. Students and landlords estimate the cost of running a washing machine, a water heater, or a computer setup. People considering a new appliance weigh its purchase price against what it will cost to run over a year, which is often the bigger number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What counts as one kilowatt-hour in everyday terms?**

It is the energy used by a 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour — or a 100-watt appliance running for ten hours. Your supplier bills you per kilowatt-hour, so it is the unit that decides what you pay.

**Why does my bill not match the calculator exactly?**

Because real bills include standing charges, taxes, and sometimes tiered rates that change as you use more. This tool estimates the cost of one appliance at a single flat rate, so treat it as a guide rather than a replica of your bill.

**Can I use this for appliances that cycle on and off, like a fridge?**

Yes, with an estimate of average running hours. A fridge compressor does not run constantly, so enter the hours it is actually cooling rather than 24 — your appliance manual or an energy monitor can give a realistic figure.

**Is the monthly figure based on 30 days?**

Yes. The monthly estimate multiplies the daily cost by 30, and the yearly figure by 365, which keeps the maths transparent and easy to check.
