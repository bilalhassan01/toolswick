## Why Client-Side Matters

Body measurements are personal health information, and they deserve to stay on your own device. A body fat estimate asks for your height and a few circumferences — details you would not want uploaded to a server or stored in someone else's database. This tool runs the entire calculation inside your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere, nothing is saved, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind. You get a private estimate without creating an account or handing over any data.

## Step-by-Step Guide

First choose your sex and your unit system — metric (centimetres) or imperial (inches). Then enter your height, your neck circumference, and your waist circumference. Women also enter a hip circumference, because the method uses it for the female calculation. The result appears instantly: an estimated body fat percentage, an estimate of your lean body mass, and a fitness category classification. You can change any measurement and watch the estimate update straight away, which makes it easy to see how a different waist measurement would change the result.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator uses the US Navy circumference method, which estimates body composition from tape measurements instead of calipers or a DEXA scan. It combines height with neck and waist measurements — plus hip measurements for women — in a formula developed for military fitness screening. The result is an estimate, not a medical measurement: studies suggest it typically lands within about 3–4% of a DEXA scan. Alongside the percentage, the tool shows your estimated lean mass (everything that is not fat) and places the result in a fitness category, so the number has some context instead of standing alone. Because the method needs only a tape measure, it is easy to repeat the same way each time, which matters more than any single reading: consistent measurements taken the same way give the most useful trend over weeks and months.

## Use Cases

People starting a fitness programme use the estimate as a baseline, then re-measure every few weeks to see whether the number is moving in the right direction. Gym-goers tracking a cut or a bulk check whether lost weight is coming from fat or from lean mass. Anyone curious about their body composition gets a rough answer in a minute with a tape measure, instead of booking a scan. Coaches and trainers use it as a quick field check with clients, since it needs no equipment beyond a tape. It also suits people who simply want to understand the difference between body weight and body fat.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need special equipment to measure myself?**

No, just a flexible tape measure. Measure over bare skin where possible, keep the tape level and snug without squeezing, and take each measurement twice to check it is consistent.

**How often should I re-check my body fat percentage?**

Every four to six weeks is a sensible rhythm. Body composition changes slowly, and measuring too often mostly shows normal day-to-day variation in the tape readings rather than real change.

**Does the calculator work for children or teenagers?**

No. The US Navy method was developed for adults, so its estimates are not reliable for younger people, and the category labels do not apply to them either.

**Is this a substitute for medical advice?**

No. It is an estimate for general fitness tracking only. If you have a health concern or are making a major change to diet or exercise, speak to a qualified health professional.
