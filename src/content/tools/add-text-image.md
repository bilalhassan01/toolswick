## Why Client-Side Matters

Photos often need a few words to make sense — a name on a team picture, a price on a product shot, or a date on a holiday memory. Most caption tools ask you to upload the photo to a server, where a copy can be stored or kept after you leave. This tool keeps your photo on your own device. The text is drawn onto the image inside your browser, so nothing is uploaded and nothing is stored elsewhere.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Adding text to a photo here takes under a minute. First, upload your photo into the caption workspace by dragging it in or clicking to browse. Your image appears right away, ready for text. Second, type your headline or caption in the text box — press Enter for extra lines if you want a second line underneath. Third, style it: pick a font family, drag the size slider, choose a text colour, set the alignment, and switch on the background highlight banner if the photo behind the words is busy. You can place the text at the top, centre, or bottom of the image. Finally, click "Download Captioned Photo" to save the finished image to your device.

## Feature Deep Dive

The captioning engine draws text directly onto your image using your browser's own font rendering, so letters stay sharp at any export size — there is no blurry re-compression of the words themselves. You control the font family, the size, the colour, and the alignment, which means the caption can match a brand style or stand out on a busy photograph. The background highlight banner is the most useful control: it places a soft, semi-transparent box behind the text, so white lettering stays readable over a bright sky and dark lettering stays readable over a shadowed street. Placement is just as flexible, with top, centre, and bottom positions for headlines, watermarks, and captions. Because everything runs in local browser memory, your original photo is never changed — the tool always produces a fresh download, and you can edit the wording and export again as many times as you like.

## Use Cases

Captioned photos show up everywhere once you start looking. Small businesses add prices, sale dates, and product names to photos before posting them, so customers get the message without opening a caption. Teachers and trainers label diagrams and class photos, and families add names and dates to pictures before printing them or sharing them in a group chat. Bloggers and social media managers use headline banners on cover images to pull readers into an article, while event organisers add the date and venue to a photo so one image carries the whole announcement. It is also handy for simple labelling — marking who is who in a group photo, or adding a short quote to a picture you want to frame. Because the text is baked into the image file itself, the message travels with the photo wherever it is shared or printed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will the text stay sharp if I share the photo at full size?**

Yes. The text is rendered with your browser's vector font engine, so the letterforms stay crisp at the exported resolution instead of turning soft or pixelated.

**Can I change the caption after I have downloaded the photo?**

Not on the downloaded file itself, because the text becomes part of the image. But your original photo is never changed, so just open it in the tool again, edit the wording, and download a fresh copy.

**Does this work on my phone as well as my computer?**

Yes. The tool runs in any modern mobile browser, so you can caption a photo straight from your phone's gallery and save the finished image without installing an app.

**Can I write the caption in another language?**

Yes. You can type any text your keyboard supports, including other languages and scripts, and it will be drawn onto the image the same way.
