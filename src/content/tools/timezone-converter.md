## Why Client-Side Matters

Scheduling a call across time zones is one of the easiest ways to get a meeting wrong — one person joins an hour early, another misses it entirely, and daylight saving changes make it worse twice a year. Many converters are buried in apps that want your calendar access or your location. This tool converts times entirely inside your browser, using the built-in timezone data your device already has. Nothing is uploaded, no account is needed, and no calendar access is requested.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Converting a time takes seconds. First, select your source time and date, along with the origin timezone it belongs to. Second, select a destination timezone or a major global city to convert to. Third, view the equivalent converted time instantly, including any date offset — so you can see at a glance when the meeting falls on the other side of the world, even if it lands on a different day. Finally, copy the converted schedule for your calendar invitation or message, so everyone you invite sees the right time.

## Feature Deep Dive

The converter is built on your browser's native timezone engine, which knows the world's time zones and their daylight saving rules without needing any external lookup. That matters because daylight saving does not change on the same dates everywhere — a meeting set for March or October can shift by an hour depending on which side of the change it falls on, and the tool handles those shifts automatically. You can add multiple comparison cities side by side, which turns a one-to-one conversion into a small world clock: pick a time in Lahore and see it at once in London, New York, and Sydney. The date offset is shown clearly, so a 9pm call in one city that lands at 2am the next day in another is never a surprise.

## Use Cases

Timezone conversion is a daily task for remote teams, who schedule stand-ups, interviews, and client calls across continents and need to know that everyone is joining at the same moment. Families with relatives abroad use it to find a good time to call without waking anyone up. Travellers check what time they will land in local time, and what time it will be back home when they arrive. Students in online courses check when a live class or an exam deadline falls in their own time zone, and event organisers publish times that make sense for an international audience instead of only their own city.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I handle a meeting where people are in three or four time zones?**

Add each city as a comparison. The tool shows the same moment in every city side by side, so you can find a slot that is reasonable for everyone at once.

**What happens when daylight saving changes mid-week?**

The browser's timezone engine applies the correct offset for the exact date you pick, so a meeting scheduled before the change and one scheduled after it will convert correctly.

**Can I convert a time in the past, like a flight that already landed?**

Yes. Pick any date and time, past or future, and the tool converts it using the timezone rules that applied on that date.

**Why does the converted time sometimes fall on a different day?**

Because the world is split into time zones up to 12 hours apart in each direction. A late-evening time in one place can already be the next morning — or still the previous evening — somewhere else.
