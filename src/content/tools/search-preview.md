## Why Client-Side Matters

A page title and description are written before the page goes live, and previewing them should not mean sending unreleased content to a server. Draft titles often reveal a product launch, a new article, or a rebrand that is not public yet. This tool simulates the search result entirely in your browser: you type the title, URL, and description, and the preview renders locally, on both desktop and mobile layouts. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is sent to a search engine, and your unpublished plans stay private.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Type your page title, the destination URL, and the meta description into the three fields. The preview updates in real time, showing how the result would look as a desktop listing and as a mobile card. Next, check the counters: the character count for the description and the pixel width meter for the title. Google truncates desktop titles at roughly 600 pixels, about 60 characters, and snippets at about 160 characters, so if the meter shows your title running past the limit, shorten it until it fits. Switch between the desktop and mobile views to confirm the snippet reads well on both, then copy the title and description into your page.

## Feature Deep Dive

The heart of the tool is the title pixel width meter. Google measures titles in pixels, not characters, using proportional type, so a title full of wide letters like W and M can be truncated at far fewer characters than a narrow one. The meter measures your exact title and warns you before it crosses the limit. The description counter tracks the 160-character snippet boundary, so you can see whether your text will be cut off with an ellipsis. Both previews render live: the desktop view shows the classic blue-link listing, while the mobile view shows the narrower card layout, where titles wrap and descriptions shorten. Because everything is local, you can test a dozen title variations in a minute without touching your live site.

## Use Cases

Bloggers preview every new article's snippet before publishing, catching a truncated title while it is still easy to fix. SEO specialists compare several title options side by side to find the one that fits and reads best. Shop owners check product page snippets so the product name and key detail survive truncation. Anyone rewriting an old page uses it to confirm the new title actually displays in full. It is also handy after a redesign, when titles that once fit may now be cut off by a changed layout.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does Google sometimes show a different title than mine?**

Google may rewrite a title if it thinks another version fits the search better, often pulling text from the page itself. The preview shows what you provided, which is the version Google starts from — a clear, fitting title is the best defence against a rewrite.

**Does this preview guarantee how Google will display my page?**

No. It is a simulation built on Google's known truncation limits, and Google can adjust how it displays any result. What it guarantees is that your title and description fit within the limits, which is the part you control.

**What URL should I enter?**

The final canonical URL of the page — the address people will land on. The preview uses it as the display link, so entering the real address shows you exactly what searchers will see.

**Can I preview rich results like star ratings or FAQ dropdowns?**

This tool previews the standard title-and-snippet result, which is what most pages get. Rich results depend on structured data on your page and on Google choosing to show it, which no preview tool can promise.
