## Why Client-Side Matters

Sleep is personal, and working out your bedtime should not require an account or a server. This tool calculates your ideal bedtimes and wake-up times entirely inside your own browser. Nothing you enter is uploaded or stored, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. You get a plan for waking up refreshed without sharing anything about your routine with anyone.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Choose whether you know your target wake-up time or your bedtime. Enter that time, and the calculator works out a set of suggested times for the other end of your sleep. It allows an average of 14 minutes to fall asleep, then counts backwards or forwards in full sleep cycles to give you several options. Pick one of the recommended times, aiming for five or six complete cycles where you can. If your schedule changes, enter a new time and the suggestions update instantly, so you can plan around early starts, late nights, and shift changes.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator is built around the natural sleep cycle, which lasts about 90 minutes and moves through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM sleep. Waking in the middle of deep sleep is what causes that heavy, groggy feeling, while waking between cycles, at the end of light sleep, leaves you feeling far more refreshed. That is why the tool suggests times in whole cycles rather than round numbers of hours: five complete cycles is seven and a half hours of sleep, and six cycles is nine hours, and both are better than an in-between figure like eight hours that can land you mid-cycle. The 14-minute allowance for falling asleep is built into every suggestion, so the bedtime it gives you is when to be in bed, not when to close your eyes.

## Use Cases

Early risers use the calculator to work backwards from an alarm time and find a bedtime that lets them wake between cycles. Shift workers use it to plan sleep around changing rosters, and students use it before exams to avoid waking groggy after a late night of revision. Parents use it to set sensible bedtimes for children and teenagers, and anyone trying to fix a broken sleep routine uses it to pick a consistent wake time and build backwards from there. It is also handy before a long flight or an early journey, when one good night makes the whole next day easier.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do I feel groggy even after eight hours of sleep?**

Because you may have woken in the middle of a deep sleep phase. Total hours matter, but so does timing: waking at the end of a complete 90-minute cycle usually feels much better than waking mid-cycle, even if the total sleep is slightly shorter.

**Should I aim for five or six cycles?**

Most healthy adults feel best after five or six complete cycles, which is seven and a half to nine hours. Try five first if you are short on time, and six when you can manage it, then see which leaves you feeling more rested.

**Does the calculator account for the time it takes to fall asleep?**

Yes. It allows an average of 14 minutes to fall asleep, so the bedtime it suggests is when you should be in bed. If you usually take much longer to drift off, go to bed a little earlier than the suggested time.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser. The times you enter are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
