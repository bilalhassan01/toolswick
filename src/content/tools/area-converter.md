## Why Client-Side Matters

Area figures turn up in property listings, floor plans, garden projects, and land records, and they often relate to a purchase or a place you live. There is no reason those numbers should travel to a server just to be converted. This tool does every calculation inside your browser, so the areas you type never leave your device, are never stored anywhere, and vanish when you close the tab. It is a simple unit conversion that stays completely private.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Type your area value into the entry box, then choose the unit you are starting from — square metres, square feet, acres, hectares, square yards, square kilometres, or square miles. Pick the unit you want to convert to, and the answer appears immediately. There is no button to press and no waiting. Below the main result, a breakdown table shows the same area in all eight units at once, so you can compare them side by side. A copy button puts the converted result on your clipboard for pasting into a document, message, or listing.

## Feature Deep Dive

Area scales with the square of linear dimensions, which is why converting it is not as simple as converting a length. Since one foot equals exactly 0.3048 metres, one square foot equals exactly 0.09290304 square metres, and the tool uses these exact relationships rather than rounded shortcuts. One international acre equals 43,560 square feet, or about 4,046.86 square metres, and one hectare equals 10,000 square metres. The breakdown table is the most useful part of the page: it converts your value into every unit simultaneously, so a single entry answers the question in metric, imperial, and land-measurement terms all at once. Square miles and square kilometres sit at the large end of the scale, for districts and countryside, while square yards are the small everyday unit still used for flooring and fabric in some countries.

## Use Cases

Home buyers and renters convert listing sizes when a property is advertised in square feet but they think in square metres, or the other way round. Gardeners and builders work out how much turf, paving, or flooring a space needs, converting a plan drawn in one unit into the unit their supplier uses. Farmers and land agents compare fields quoted in acres against plots quoted in hectares. Students and teachers use the converter for geography and maths work, checking land areas across countries that use different systems. Anyone reading a foreign property advert gets an instant sense of scale without doing the arithmetic by hand.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which unit should I use for a house floor plan?**

Square metres in most of the world, and square feet in the United States. The breakdown table shows both, so you can quote whichever your audience expects.

**How big is a square kilometre in everyday terms?**

One square kilometre equals exactly 1,000,000 square metres — roughly 140 football pitches. It is a large unit, used for towns, districts, and countryside rather than individual plots.

**Can I convert very small areas, like a single tile?**

Yes. The tool accepts decimal values, so you can enter a fraction of a square metre or square foot and see the result in every unit, including square yards.

**Why do acre and hectare figures sometimes look rounded?**

Because the exact conversion is not a round number: one hectare is about 2.47105 acres. The tool shows the full converted value rather than a rough estimate.
