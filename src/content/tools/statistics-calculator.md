## Why Client-Side Matters

Statistics often means pasting in a set of numbers that belong to your work, your class, or your research, and that data should not travel to a server just to find an average. This tool calculates every statistic entirely inside your own browser. Your dataset never leaves your device, nothing is uploaded or stored, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. You get a full summary without sharing your numbers with anyone.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Paste your numbers into the data field, separated by commas or spaces. Then click "Calculate Statistics" and the tool works out the full summary at once. You will see the mean, median, and mode, along with the minimum, maximum, and range. Below that, the tool shows the sample standard deviation, the population variance, and the quartiles, giving you a complete picture of the spread as well as the centre. Paste a new set of numbers and click again to analyse a different dataset.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator covers the measures people actually use to describe a dataset. The mean is the ordinary average, the median is the middle value when the numbers are sorted, and the mode is the value that appears most often; together they show where the data sits. The range, minimum, and maximum show the extremes. Standard deviation and variance measure the spread: a small standard deviation means the numbers cluster tightly around the mean, while a large one means they are widely scattered. The tool shows both the sample and population versions, since the sample version divides by one less than the count and the population version divides by the count itself. Quartiles split the sorted data into quarters, so you can see where the middle half of your numbers falls.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between mean and median?**

The mean is the average of all values added together and divided by the count, while the median is the middle value when the numbers are sorted. The median is less affected by extreme outliers, so it often describes typical values better when a few very large or small numbers skew the average.

**What are quartiles?**

Quartiles split sorted data into four equal parts. The first quartile marks the point below which a quarter of the values fall, the second quartile is the median, and the third quartile marks the point below which three quarters fall. Together they show where the middle half of your data sits.

**Can I paste numbers with decimals or negative values?**

Yes. The data field accepts decimal and negative numbers, separated by commas or spaces, and every statistic is calculated from the exact values you entered.

**Is my data sent anywhere?**

No. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser. The numbers you paste are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
