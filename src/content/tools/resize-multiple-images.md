## Why Client-Side Matters

Preparing images for a website, a listing, or a presentation usually means resizing the same photo over and over — and doing that for dozens of pictures by hand takes hours. Most batch tools upload your whole folder to a server, where your private photos sit on someone else's machine. This tool resizes every image inside your own browser, using the canvas built into the page. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and the resized files download straight back to you.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Resizing a batch of images takes a few minutes. First, drag and drop multiple images into the workspace, or choose files from your folder — JPG, PNG, and WebP all work in one batch. Second, select a target width, height, or scaling mode, and decide whether to maintain the aspect ratio so photos are not stretched. Third, click "Resize All Images" and let the tool process the whole batch. Finally, download the results: save individual images one by one, or grab everything together in a single ZIP archive.

## Feature Deep Dive

The resizer gives you two ways to set the new size: exact dimensions in pixels, or a percentage of the original, which is handy when you want every photo at half or quarter size without working out the numbers. The aspect-ratio option is the one to watch — keep it on and the tool scales width and height together, so a photo keeps its natural shape; turn it off only when you need an exact frame and are happy to crop or stretch. Processing happens image by image in browser memory, so you can resize dozens of photos at once, limited only by your device's RAM. Each image keeps its original name in the ZIP, so the resized set drops neatly back into the folder it came from, and your originals are never modified — the tool always produces fresh files.

## Use Cases

Batch resizing is a staple job for website owners, who shrink product photos and blog images to consistent widths so pages load fast and layouts stay tidy. Photographers prepare whole galleries at a smaller size for client previews, while keeping full-size originals untouched. Sellers resize listing photos to a marketplace's preferred dimensions, and office workers shrink screenshots and scanned pages so they fit into slides and documents. It is also useful before travel: resizing a folder of photos to a smaller size means they take up less space on a phone and upload faster when shared from a slow connection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I resize by exact dimensions or by percentage?**

Use exact dimensions when a platform asks for a specific size, such as 1200 pixels wide. Use percentage when you just want everything smaller by the same amount, like half size for emailing.

**What happens if I turn off "maintain aspect ratio"?**

The image is forced to the exact width and height you set, which can stretch or squash the photo. Keep the option on unless you specifically need a fixed frame.

**Can I resize a mix of JPG, PNG, and WebP files together?**

Yes. The batch resizer accepts all three formats in one run, and each image is processed the same way regardless of its format.

**Do the resized images keep their original names?**

Yes. Each file keeps its original name in the ZIP download, so you can tell which resized image came from which original at a glance.
