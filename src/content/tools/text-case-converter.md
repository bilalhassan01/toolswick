## Why Client-Side Matters

Capitalisation looks trivial until it goes wrong: a headline in the wrong case, a variable name that breaks a build, or a database field that does not match the rest of the system. Fixing it by hand means retyping, and retyping means typos. Sending your text to an online converter means sending whatever you pasted — sometimes code, sometimes draft copy, sometimes something private — to a server you do not control. This tool converts case entirely in your browser with plain JavaScript, so nothing is uploaded and nothing is stored. The conversion is instant, there is no account to create, and your text never leaves the page you are working on.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Converting text takes three clicks. First, paste or type your text into the input box — a headline, a sentence, a variable name, or a whole paragraph. Second, click any of the eight conversion buttons: UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, Sentence case, camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case, or kebab-case. The converted result appears immediately in the output box below. Third, click Copy Output to put the converted text on your clipboard, ready to paste wherever it belongs. If you want to try a different case on the same text, just click another button — the original input stays put, so you can compare results without retyping. The Clear button empties both boxes when you are ready for the next piece of text.

## Feature Deep Dive

The eight formats cover the cases people actually need. UPPERCASE and lowercase change every letter and leave everything else alone, which is ideal for fixing a caps-lock accident or normalising pasted text. Title Case capitalises the first letter of every word, the standard look for headlines and titles. Sentence case capitalises only the first letter of each sentence, matching ordinary prose. The four programming formats do more than change letters: camelCase and PascalCase strip spaces and punctuation to join words together, with camelCase starting lowercase and PascalCase starting uppercase, while snake_case and kebab-case join words with underscores or hyphens and force everything lowercase. Because each button is a separate transformation applied to the same input, you can switch between formats freely and see which one fits — a variable name, a file name, or a heading — without losing the original text. The tool never stores what you paste, so draft copy, code, and private text are safe to convert and then clear.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between Title Case and Sentence case?**

Title Case capitalises the first letter of every word, which suits headlines. Sentence case capitalises only the first letter of each sentence, which matches ordinary prose.

**Does Title Case leave small words like "and" lowercase?**

No. This tool capitalises every word in Title Case, including short words such as "and", "the", and "of", so headlines come out uniformly capitalised.

**Which case should I use for a file name or a URL?**

Use kebab-case, with hyphens between words, for file names and URLs. Use snake_case, with underscores, for code identifiers and database fields.

**Can I convert a long document in one go?**

Yes. Paste the whole document into the input box and click a case button — the conversion runs instantly on the full text, and you can copy the result out in one go.

**Does converting change my punctuation or line breaks?**

It depends on the format. UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, and Sentence case only change letter casing. CamelCase, PascalCase, snake_case, and kebab-case also remove spaces and punctuation to join the words together.
