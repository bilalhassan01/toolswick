## Why Client-Side Matters

An investment projection involves your money — your starting balance, your monthly contributions, and the future you are planning for. Those figures should not be sent to a server to be crunched. This tool runs the entire calculation inside your browser, so everything you type never leaves your device, is never stored, and vanishes when you close the tab. You get a full long-term projection without an account or any financial data leaving your machine.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Enter your initial investment balance, then the amount you plan to add each month. Next, enter the expected annual rate of return and the investment horizon in years. The results appear instantly: the projected portfolio value at the end of the period, the total amount you contributed, and the total interest earned. A breakdown separates your own contributions from the growth, so you can see exactly how much of the final figure came from your deposits and how much came from compounding.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator compounds returns monthly, matching the rhythm of regular monthly contributions. Each month, your new contribution is added and the whole balance grows by one month's share of the annual return, so growth builds on growth — the compounding effect that makes long horizons so powerful. The breakdown is the most revealing part: over a long period, the interest earned can grow to rival or even exceed the total you put in, which is why starting early matters more than starting big. The projection assumes a steady rate of return throughout, which keeps the maths transparent and lets you test different rates side by side.

## Use Cases

People starting a retirement plan see what decades of small monthly contributions could grow into, and what happens if they start five years later. Savers working towards a house deposit test whether their monthly amount will reach the target in time. Parents planning for a child's education compare different contribution levels to find one the family budget can sustain. Anyone comparing two return assumptions — say, 5% versus 7% — sees how a small difference compounds into a large gap over twenty years. It is also a useful reality check before committing to a long-term savings habit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What rate of return should I enter?**

Use a cautious, realistic figure rather than the best year you have seen. Historical long-term averages for diversified investments are often quoted around a few percent a year after inflation, but no rate is guaranteed — try a low, a middle, and a high figure to see the range of outcomes.

**Does the projection account for inflation?**

No. The figures are in nominal terms, meaning future money is shown at face value. A sum that looks large in thirty years will buy less than the same sum today, so keep inflation in mind when judging whether the target is enough.

**Are fees or taxes included?**

No. Investment fees, account charges, and taxes all reduce the real outcome, and they differ by provider and country. The projection shows the gross figure; subtract your expected costs to get a more honest picture.

**What if I stop contributing halfway through?**

The projection assumes steady monthly contributions for the whole horizon. To model stopping early, run the calculation for the contribution period, then use that result as the starting balance with zero contributions for the remaining years.
