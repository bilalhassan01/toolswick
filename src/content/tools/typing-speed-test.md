## Why Client-Side Matters

Typing speed is one of those quiet skills that shapes your whole working day — faster, more accurate typing means less time on every email, document, and message. Most typing tests are wrapped in ads, accounts, or courses trying to sell you something. This one runs entirely in your browser: a 60-second test with real-time prompts, live word highlighting, and your words per minute at the end. Nothing is installed, nothing is uploaded, and your results stay on your own device.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Taking the test takes just over a minute. First, click into the typing input box to start the test timer — the clock begins when you start typing. Second, type the displayed words as accurately and quickly as you can, following the highlighted text. Third, correct mistakes as you go, or press space to proceed to the next word. Finally, when the time runs out, view your final results: Gross WPM, Net WPM, accuracy percentage, and keystroke count.

## Feature Deep Dive

The test measures two different speeds, and the difference between them is what makes the results useful. Gross WPM counts everything you typed, including mistakes, while Net WPM subtracts the errors — so the gap between the two tells you whether your speed is real or whether you are typing fast and wrong. Accuracy is shown as a percentage, and the keystroke count records exactly how much you typed. The standard formula is simple: WPM equals all typed characters divided by five, divided by the time in minutes — five characters counting as one word. Average typing speed is roughly 40 WPM, while competitive typists exceed 80 to 100 WPM. The live word highlighting shows your place in the text and flags mistakes as they happen, so you can correct them immediately instead of discovering them at the end.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between Gross WPM and Net WPM?**

Gross WPM counts everything you typed, while Net WPM subtracts your errors. If the two numbers are far apart, you are typing fast but making many mistakes — slowing down slightly usually raises your Net WPM.

**How can I improve my typing speed?**

Practise regularly in short sessions, focus on accuracy first, and use the correct finger positions. Speed follows accuracy — fast typing full of errors is slower overall once corrections are counted.

**Does the test use the same words every time?**

The prompts vary, so each test feels fresh. That keeps the test honest — you are typing new words each time, not memorising one passage.

**What is a good accuracy score to aim for?**

Aim for 95 percent or higher. High accuracy with moderate speed beats high speed with many errors, because Net WPM punishes mistakes.
