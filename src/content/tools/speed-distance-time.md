## Why Client-Side Matters

Working out a journey time, a running pace, or a delivery speed should be a quick private calculation, not a round trip to a server. This tool solves speed, distance, and time entirely inside your own browser, the moment you type. Nothing you enter is uploaded or stored, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. It is a simple piece of trip maths, and it stays on your device.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start by choosing what you want to calculate: speed, distance, or time. Then enter the two values you already know, choosing the unit for each one, such as miles or kilometres for distance and hours or minutes for time. The missing value is calculated automatically in real time as you type, with no button to press. Below the answer you will see the result converted into other units, so a distance appears in both miles and kilometres and a time in both hours and minutes. Change any figure and the answer updates instantly.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator is built on a single relationship: distance equals speed multiplied by time. Pick any one of the three to solve for, and the tool rearranges the formula to find it from the other two. The real strength is the unit handling: you can enter a distance in miles and a time in minutes and ask for speed in kilometres per hour, and the tool converts everything behind the scenes. Speed can be shown in miles per hour, kilometres per hour, or knots; distance in miles or kilometres; and time in hours or minutes. That flexibility matters because real trips mix units constantly, from a speedometer in miles per hour to a map measured in kilometres.

## Use Cases

Drivers use the calculator to plan road trips, working out arrival times from distance and average speed, or checking how fast they need to travel to arrive on time. Runners and cyclists use it to turn a target time into a required pace, or to see what speed a personal best actually means. Students use it for physics problems, where the same formula appears again and again with different unknowns. Delivery drivers, pilots, and sailors use it to estimate journey times, and anyone planning a walk, a hike, or a commute uses it to turn a distance on a map into a realistic time on the road.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if I only know my average speed roughly?**

Use your best estimate and treat the answer as a guide. Traffic, stops, and hills all change real journey times, so a rough speed gives a rough time. Try a slower speed as well to see the latest you might arrive.

**Can I work out how long a walk will take?**

Yes. Choose time as the value to calculate, enter the walking distance and your walking speed, and the tool gives the duration. A typical walking speed is around 5 kilometres per hour, or about 3 miles per hour, on flat ground.

**Does the calculator account for stops or traffic?**

No. It calculates the time for steady travel at the speed you enter. For a real journey, add extra time for fuel stops, rest breaks, and traffic, especially on long trips.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser. The numbers you enter are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
