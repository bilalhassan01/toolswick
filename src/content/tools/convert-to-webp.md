## Why Client-Side Matters

Converting images to WebP usually means uploading them to a website first, and that is a poor trade when the images are product photos, client work, or personal pictures. An upload creates a copy on a server you do not control, even if only briefly. This tool converts entirely inside your browser, in local memory, through WebAssembly and Canvas APIs. Your JPG and PNG files never leave your computer or phone. No file data, no filenames, and no metadata are ever transmitted, so converting a batch of product shots or family photos is as private as editing them on your own device.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Converting to WebP takes three quick steps. First, drop your JPG or PNG images onto the tool, or click to browse and select them from your computer or phone. You can convert several images in one batch. Second, adjust the WebP quality slider. A setting of 80 is recommended for websites: it keeps the image looking sharp while cutting the file size substantially. Move it higher for maximum quality, or lower it when every kilobyte counts. Third, download your ultra-compact WebP files, either one by one or all together. The converted files are ready to use immediately — upload them to your website, attach them to a listing, or share them anywhere WebP is accepted.

## Feature Deep Dive

WebP is a modern image format built for the web, and its advantage is simple: the same picture at the same visual quality takes up 25 to 35 percent less space than a JPG. That saving comes from more efficient compression, not from throwing away detail you can see. For a website, the effect multiplies across every image on every page — faster loads, less bandwidth, and better scores on Core Web Vitals, the loading measures Google uses when ranking pages.

WebP is flexible in ways JPG is not. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, so you can choose small files or perfect fidelity. Unlike JPG, it supports full 8-bit alpha channel transparency, which means logos and graphics with transparent backgrounds convert cleanly instead of gaining a white box. The format is supported by all modern browsers, so a WebP image displays correctly for virtually every visitor. The conversion itself runs locally in your browser, so a batch of images converts quickly with no upload queue and no server limit.The saving from WebP compounds across a website. A single image that is 30 percent smaller is a modest win; a hundred images that are 30 percent smaller is a page that loads noticeably faster on every visit, on every device, and on every connection. That is why the format has become the default for image-heavy sites. The quality slider is the main control: 80 is the recommended balance for the web, higher preserves more detail for hero images and photography, and lower is fine for thumbnails and decorative graphics where nobody looks closely. Because conversion happens locally, you can convert a whole batch of images in one session and upload them all at once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much smaller are WebP files than JPG?**

WebP cuts image sizes by 25 to 35 percent compared to JPG at identical visual quality. The exact saving depends on the image, but the difference is substantial across a whole website.

**Will converting to WebP change my image dimensions?**

No. Conversion changes the format and the file size, not the pixel dimensions. A converted image is the same width and height as the original.

**Will WebP images work in all browsers?**

WebP is supported by all modern browsers, so it displays correctly for virtually every visitor. Very old browsers may not support it, but they are rare today.

**Are my images uploaded to a server?**

No. Conversion happens entirely in your browser's local memory. Your images never leave your device, and no file data or metadata is transmitted.

**Can I convert WebP back to JPG or PNG?**

Yes. Use the WebP to JPG and WebP to PNG tools on this site to convert in the other direction, for the rare case where a destination does not accept WebP.
