## Why Client-Side Matters

Power figures turn up on appliance labels, motor plates, generators, and solar panels, and converting them should be a quick private check, not a round trip to a server. This tool does the whole conversion inside your own browser, the moment you type. The watts, kilowatts, or horsepower you enter never leave your device, nothing is stored anywhere, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. It is a simple unit conversion, and there is no reason for it to travel further than your screen.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start by typing your power value into the number box. Then choose the unit you are converting from, such as watts, kilowatts, megawatts, horsepower, or BTU per hour. Next, choose the unit you want to convert to, and the answer appears immediately, with no button to press. Below the result you will see a full comparison table showing the same value expressed in every supported unit at once, so you can scan across units without converting again. If you change any number or switch either unit, the result and the table update instantly.

## Feature Deep Dive

The converter covers the power units people actually meet in real life. Watts and kilowatts are the everyday electrical units, megawatts suit generators and power plants, horsepower is still the standard for engines and motors, and BTU per hour is common in heating and cooling equipment. One mechanical horsepower equals about 745.7 watts, while metric horsepower is slightly different at about 735.5 watts, and the tool keeps those definitions straight so you do not have to remember them. The comparison table is the most useful part: enter a figure once and see it in every unit side by side, which makes it easy to compare a motor rated in horsepower with a heater rated in watts, or to check whether a generator's kilowatt rating matches what an appliance needs.

## Use Cases

Electricians and engineers use the converter to translate motor and equipment ratings between the units on a nameplate and the units in a specification. Homeowners use it to compare appliances, work out what a generator can actually run, or make sense of a solar panel or inverter rating. Heating and cooling work is another common case: air conditioners and boilers are often rated in BTU per hour, while electrical supply is measured in watts or kilowatts, and converting between the two is the only way to check they match. Students use it for physics homework, and anyone buying a motor, pump, or generator uses it to compare products that manufacturers have rated in different units.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I convert kilowatts to BTU per hour?**

Yes. Enter the kilowatt value, choose kilowatts as the starting unit and BTU per hour as the target, and the result appears at once. The comparison table also shows the same value in every other unit, so you can check several conversions in one glance.

**What is the difference between power and energy?**

Power is the rate at which energy is used or produced, while energy is power multiplied by time. A kilowatt measures power; a kilowatt-hour measures the energy used by running one kilowatt for one hour. This tool converts power units, not energy units.

**Does the converter work without an internet connection?**

Yes, once the page has loaded. All of the conversion math runs inside your browser, so you can keep converting even if your connection drops. Nothing is fetched from a server while you use it.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. Every conversion runs entirely on your device. The numbers you enter are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
