## Why Client-Side Matters

A calorie calculation asks for your age, sex, height, weight, and activity level — personal health details that should not travel to a server just to produce a number. This tool does the whole calculation inside your browser. Your details are never uploaded, never stored, and never shared; they disappear when you close the tab. You get daily calorie targets and a macro split without an account, a subscription, or a privacy trade-off.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Enter your age, sex, height, and current weight, then choose metric or imperial units so the boxes match the numbers you know. Next, pick the activity level that best describes a typical week, from sedentary (little or no exercise) through light and moderate to heavy exercise six or seven days a week. The results appear instantly: your BMR, your daily maintenance calories (TDEE), and targets for weight loss or muscle gain, plus a suggested split of protein, carbohydrate, and fat in grams. Change any input and the targets update at once.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, a widely used formula for estimating Basal Metabolic Rate — the calories your body burns at rest to keep vital functions going. Your BMR is then multiplied by an activity factor to give TDEE, the calories needed to maintain your current weight. Weight-loss targets come from a daily deficit against that maintenance figure, and muscle-gain targets from a small surplus. The macro split divides your maintenance calories into roughly 30% protein, 40% carbohydrate, and 30% fat, converted into grams using 4 calories per gram of protein or carbohydrate and 9 per gram of fat. Because the split is calculated from your maintenance figure, it moves with your activity level too — a heavier training week raises the total, and the grams rise with it.

## Use Cases

People starting a weight-loss plan use the maintenance figure as their anchor, then aim for the suggested deficit instead of guessing a random calorie number. Those building muscle check the surplus target and the protein figure to make sure they are eating enough to support training. Runners and gym-goers compare targets across activity levels to see how a busier week changes their needs. Anyone curious about their own metabolism gets a quick, evidence-based estimate in under a minute. Meal planners use the macro grams as a starting point for building a day of eating around the targets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does my activity level change the result so much?**

Because activity is the biggest variable after body size. A heavily active person can burn hundreds of calories more per day than a sedentary person of the same weight, so picking the honest level matters more than getting your weight exactly right.

**Are the macro grams a strict prescription?**

No. The 30/40/30 split is a general starting point for a balanced diet, not a personalised nutrition plan. People with specific goals or health conditions should adjust it with a qualified professional.

**Can I use this calculator while pregnant or breastfeeding?**

The standard formulas do not account for pregnancy or breastfeeding, which change calorie needs. Use it only as a rough reference and ask a healthcare professional for targets that fit your situation.

**How often should I recalculate my targets?**

Whenever your weight changes by a few kilos, or your activity level shifts for more than a couple of weeks. As your weight falls, your maintenance calories fall too, so the old target gradually becomes too generous.
