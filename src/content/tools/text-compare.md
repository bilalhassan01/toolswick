## Why Client-Side Matters

Text you compare is often text you cannot afford to leak. Contracts, draft agreements, unpublished manuscripts, source code, internal policies, and exam answers all pass through comparison tools, and most online diff tools ask you to upload both versions to a server first. This tool works differently. Both texts are compared entirely inside your own browser, on your device, using a line-based comparison that never sends anything anywhere. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing remains behind after you close the tab. Your drafts stay yours.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Comparing two texts here takes less than a minute, and every step happens on the same page. First, paste the original version into the Text A box on the left. Second, paste the revised or comparison version into the Text B box on the right. Third, click the Compare Texts button below the two boxes. The tool immediately shows a statistics bar summarising how the two versions relate, followed by a line-by-line comparison view underneath. Lines that appear in both texts are shown in grey, lines found only in Text A are highlighted in green, and lines found only in Text B are highlighted in red. You can scroll through the comparison view to review every difference in order, from the first changed line to the last.

## Feature Deep Dive

The comparison works line by line. Each text is split into individual lines, and the tool checks which lines appear in both versions and which appear in only one. The result is a clear visual map of what changed: shared lines sit quietly in grey, while additions and removals stand out in green and red with a coloured bar at the edge of each line. The statistics bar above the comparison gives you the headline numbers at a glance, so you can tell in seconds whether two versions are nearly identical or substantially different, before you read through the detail.

Because the comparison runs entirely in your browser, it works the same way whether you are on a fast connection or a slow one; once the page has loaded, no network is used at all. There is no document upload step and no waiting for a server to process your files. The tool handles prose, code snippets, and configuration files equally well, since it compares lines of text rather than understanding the language they are written in.

## Use Cases

Comparing text is one of those small tasks that comes up constantly once you notice it. Editors and writers use it to check a revised draft against the original, catching sentences that were changed, moved, or dropped during editing. Lawyers and contract managers compare clauses between two versions of an agreement to confirm exactly what was altered before signing. Developers compare two versions of a code snippet or a configuration file to spot what changed between releases, which is often faster than reading both files from top to bottom.

It is just as useful for everyday checks. Students compare an essay draft with a revised version to see whether their edits actually landed. Translators check a translated text against the source to confirm nothing was skipped. Anyone who has ever received a document back with changes and wondered what exactly was different will find the side-by-side view reassuring: instead of trusting a summary, you can see every line that changed with your own eyes, and you can do it without sending either version to a third party.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does it show changes inside a line, or only whole lines?**

The comparison works at line level. If a single word inside a line changes, the whole line is flagged as different, in green for Text A or red for Text B.

**What happens if I paste the same text into both boxes?**

Every line will match, so the whole comparison shows in grey and the statistics will confirm the two versions are identical. That is a quick way to verify that two copies of a document really are the same.

**Can I compare files, or only pasted text?**

The tool works with text you paste into the two boxes. If your text lives in a file, open the file, copy its contents, and paste them in. There is no file upload step, which is part of why your text never leaves your device.

**Does it work with very long documents?**

Yes. The comparison view scrolls, so long texts are handled the same way as short ones.

**Will my text be saved or shared anywhere?**

No. The comparison runs entirely in your browser. Your text is never uploaded, stored, or shared, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
