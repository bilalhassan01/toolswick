## Why Client-Side Matters

Percentages show up in decisions that involve your money and your data: discounts, tips, tax, salary changes, test scores, and price rises. Most online calculators are harmless enough, but there is no reason a simple percentage question should travel to a server at all. This tool works differently. All three calculations run entirely inside your own browser, on your device, updating the moment you type. Nothing you enter is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing remains behind after you close the tab. It is a small, private calculation, and it stays that way.

## Step-by-Step Guide

There are three calculation cards on the page, and each one answers a different everyday question. To work out a share of a number, use the first card: type the percentage into the first box and the number into the second, and the result appears at once. To find what share one number is of another, use the second card: type the part into the first box and the whole into the second, and the tool shows the percentage. To measure change over time, use the third card: type the old value and the new value, and the tool shows the percentage increase or decrease. Every result updates in real time as you type, so there is no button to press and no waiting. You can change any number and watch the answer adjust instantly.

## Feature Deep Dive

The three cards cover the percentage questions people actually ask. "What is X percent of Y?" is the discount, tip, and tax question: it multiplies the percentage by the number and divides by one hundred. "X is what percent of Y?" is the share question: it divides the part by the whole and converts the result to a percentage, which is how you check a test score, a survey result, or a budget share. "Percentage change" compares an old value with a new one, showing how much something rose or fell in percentage terms, and it labels the result as an increase or a decrease so the direction is clear at a glance.

The calculator accepts decimals in every box, so you can enter figures like twelve point five percent or a price with cents and still get an exact answer. If a calculation cannot be defined, the tool says so plainly instead of guessing: asking what share a number is of zero, for instance, shows "Not defined", because dividing by zero has no meaningful answer.

## Use Cases

Percentages are everywhere once you start looking. Shoppers use the first card to check a sale price, working out what a twenty-percent discount actually takes off before they reach the till. Diners use it to calculate a tip, and freelancers use it to add tax to an invoice. Students and teachers use the second card to turn raw marks into percentages, and managers use it to see what share of a budget, a target, or a survey response a figure represents.

The change card is the one people reach for when something moves. A salary rise, a rent increase, a price jump, or a fall in costs all make more sense as a percentage than as a raw number: a fifty-unit rise means something very different on a base of one hundred than on a base of ten thousand. Investors, shop owners, and anyone tracking a budget use it to compare changes fairly across different starting points, and to explain those changes to others in a single clear figure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does the calculator show when the second number is zero?**

It shows "Not defined". Asking what share a number is of zero has no meaningful answer, because dividing by zero is not possible, so the tool says so plainly instead of inventing a figure.

**Can I use decimals in the calculation?**

Yes. Every input box accepts decimal numbers, so you can enter values like twelve point five percent, or prices with cents, and the result is calculated from the exact figures you typed.

**How do I work out a discount or a tip?**

Use the first card, "What is X% of Y?". For a discount, enter the discount percentage and the original price; the result is the amount taken off.

**Does the percentage change card show whether something went up or down?**

Yes. The result is labelled as an increase or a decrease, with a plus sign for a rise, so you can tell the direction at a glance without doing any extra arithmetic.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. All three calculations run entirely in your browser. The numbers you enter are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
