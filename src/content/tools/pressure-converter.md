Next, pick the unit you want to convert to, and the converted value appears immediately. A full comparison table sits below the result, showing your value in every supported unit at once, so you can check several conversions without starting over. If you need the result elsewhere, copy it from the output box, and change any input at any time to see the answer update in real time.

## Feature Deep Dive

The converter covers the pressure units people actually encounter. Pascals and kilopascals are the scientific standard, with one pascal defined as one newton of force spread over one square metre. Bar is the common workshop and diving unit, PSI dominates in the United States for tyres and plumbing, atmospheres describe pressure relative to sea level, and Torr is used in vacuum and laboratory work. Standard atmospheric pressure at sea level is exactly 101,325 pascals, which is the same as 1.01325 bar or about 14.696 PSI, and the tool uses these exact definitions so conversions stay consistent. The comparison table is the fastest way to work: enter a value once and read it in every unit, which is handy when a manual gives one unit and your gauge reads another. Kilopascals are the unit most car manuals and weather stations use in metric countries, and they convert cleanly from bar by multiplying by one hundred.

## Use Cases

Drivers use the converter to translate tyre pressures between bar and PSI, since car manuals and tyre gauges often disagree on units. Divers and engineers convert between bar and atmospheres when planning dives or checking equipment ratings. Weather watchers use it to make sense of pressure readings in hectopascals and millibars. In workshops and factories it settles arguments between metric and imperial gauges, and in laboratories it converts Torr readings into pascals or bar for reports. Anyone following a recipe or manual written in a different measurement system uses it to keep pressures exact rather than guessed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Torr used for?**

Torr is mainly used in vacuum and laboratory work, where pressures are far below atmospheric pressure. One Torr is roughly the pressure produced by a one-millimetre column of mercury, and it is a convenient unit when pascals would give awkwardly small numbers.

**Which pressure unit do weather forecasts use?**

Most forecasts use hectopascals, which are the same as millibars. One hectopascal equals 100 pascals, so standard sea-level pressure of 101,325 pascals is reported as about 1013 hectopascals.

**Can I convert very small or very large pressures?**

Yes. The input box accepts decimals, so you can enter tiny laboratory values or large industrial pressures, and the result is calculated from the exact figures you typed.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. Every conversion runs entirely in your browser. The numbers you enter are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
