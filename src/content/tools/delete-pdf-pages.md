## Why Client-Side Matters

PDFs picked up along the way often carry pages you do not want to share: blank separator sheets, a cover page from a template, or a confidential appendix that was fine for internal use but should never leave the office. Most online page-removal tools ask you to upload the whole document to a server you do not control, where a copy can sit in storage or pass through logs. This tool works differently. Your PDF is edited entirely inside your own browser using the pdf-lib engine, in memory, on your device. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing remains behind after you close the tab.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Removing pages here takes less than a minute, and every step happens on the same page. First, drop your PDF into the workspace, or click to browse and select it from your computer or phone. The tool shows a card for every page, so you can see the document's contents at a glance. Second, choose the pages to remove: click on a page card to toggle it for deletion, or type a page range such as 2 or 4-6 for longer stretches. Selected cards are clearly marked, so you can check your choices before anything is removed. Third, click "Delete Pages & Download" to generate the cleaned document and save it to your device.

## Feature Deep Dive

The deletion engine is pdf-lib, an open-source library that runs in your browser and rebuilds the PDF from the pages you keep. The tool copies every remaining page into a new document exactly as it was — text stays selectable, links stay intact, and there is no re-rendering or quality loss, because nothing is converted to an image and back. The result is a clean, standard PDF that opens in any reader.

The visual page cards make the job hard to get wrong. Instead of guessing page numbers from a list, you look at each page and click the ones to remove, which matters when the page you want gone is a blank sheet or a wrongly scanned duplicate. For long documents, typing a range is faster: entering 2 or 4-6 removes those pages in one step. A valid PDF needs at least one page, so the tool will not let you delete every page. There is no server-imposed limit — the only practical limit is your device's browser memory — and your original file is never changed.
## Use Cases

Stripping unwanted pages is a small job that comes up constantly. Anyone sending a bank statement to a landlord or a lender usually wants the summary pages only, not the marketing inserts and terms sheets that came with them. Job seekers trim cover sheets and duplicate pages from application documents before sending a single clean file. Students remove blank pages and filler from scanned lecture notes so the file is shorter and easier to search.

It is also the simplest way to fix a bad scan. A document scanned in a hurry often includes the scanner lid, a blank page between sections, or a page scanned twice by mistake — all of which disappear in a few clicks here. A downloaded form pack may include instruction pages you do not need to submit, and deleting them leaves exactly the pages the recipient asked for. Because the remaining pages are copied exactly, the cleaned file is suitable for formal submissions, and since the original stays untouched, there is no risk in trying.
## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I remove several pages at once by typing their numbers?**

Yes. Instead of clicking each card, type a range such as 2 or 4-6 and the tool removes all of those pages at once. This is the fastest option for long documents.

**Does deleting pages change the pages that stay?**

No. The pages you keep are copied into the new document exactly as they were, so their text, images, and formatting are untouched. Nothing is re-rendered or compressed during the process.

**Will the file get smaller after I remove pages?**

Usually, yes. The removed pages and their content are left out of the new document entirely, so a file that loses several image-heavy pages can shrink noticeably.

**Can I preview a page before I delete it?**

Yes. Every page is shown as a visual card in the workspace, so you can look at the page itself — not just its number — before deciding to remove it.

**What if I delete the wrong page by accident?**

Nothing is lost. Your original file is never modified, so you can run it through the tool again and make the correct selection. You can also toggle a selected card off before you click download.
