## Why Client-Side Matters

Some decisions are genuinely 50/50 — who goes first, which film to watch, heads or tails on the toss. A physical coin works, but it is rarely in your pocket when you need it, and a coin-flip app often comes with ads or tracking. This tool flips a virtual 3D coin entirely in your browser, with a smooth animation, a running history, and fair odds. Nothing is installed, nothing is uploaded, and the result is ready in a second.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Flipping a coin takes one click. First, click "Flip Coin" or press the Spacebar — whichever is easier. Second, watch the smooth 3D coin flip animation as the coin spins through the air. Third, read the outcome when it lands: Heads or Tails. Fourth, check the tally below the coin: total flips, heads count, tails count, and the streak, so you can see the balance of your flips over time.

## Feature Deep Dive

The flip is decided by cryptographic random integers from your browser, which means the outcome is genuinely unpredictable — a true 50/50 chance on every flip, with no pattern to learn and no bias toward either side. The 3D animation is more than decoration: watching the coin spin builds the same small moment of suspense as a real toss, which is what makes a coin flip feel fair to both sides of a decision. The history tracker records every flip, so you can see the running totals and the current streak — useful when someone claims the coin is rigged, because the numbers over many flips should settle close to even. The Spacebar shortcut makes it easy to flip again and again without reaching for the mouse, which is handy when you are settling a whole series of small decisions in a row.

## Use Cases

Coin flips settle the small arguments of everyday life: who kicks off the game, who does the dishes, which team bats first, or which of two films to watch. Teachers use them to demonstrate probability in class, flipping again and again to show how the results even out over time. Game masters and board game players use them when a rule calls for a random choice, and friends use them to pick a restaurant or a route when nobody can decide. They are also a fair way to assign turns in a group — flip for who goes first, then rotate — so nobody feels the choice was rigged.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I trust that the flip is really random?**

Yes. The result comes from your browser's cryptographic random number generator, which produces unpredictable values — the digital equivalent of a well-balanced coin.

**What should I do if the same side comes up many times in a row?**

Nothing — that is normal randomness. Streaks happen with real coins too, and over many flips the totals settle close to even. A streak does not mean the next flip is "due" to change.

**Can I flip the coin without using the mouse?**

Yes. Press the Spacebar to flip, which is handy when you want to flip quickly several times in a row.

**Does the tool remember my flips if I close the page?**

The history shown is for your current session. If you close the tab, the tally resets, so note down the totals if you need them for a longer game.
