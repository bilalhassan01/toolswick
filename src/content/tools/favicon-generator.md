## Why Client-Side Matters

A favicon is the tiny icon that identifies your site in browser tabs, bookmarks, history, and phone home screens. It is small — often just 16 pixels square — but it is the first piece of branding a visitor sees, and a site without one shows a generic blank icon instead. The catch is that every platform wants a different size: browsers, desktop shortcuts, Apple devices, and Android each ask for their own dimensions. Making five separate icons by hand is fiddly, and uploading your logo to a server-based generator hands your brand artwork to a stranger's computer. This tool generates the full set entirely in your browser, from a single square graphic, with nothing uploaded and nothing stored.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Generating a favicon set takes under a minute. First, upload your logo or square graphic into the generator by dragging it in or clicking to browse. The tool automatically scales it into the five standard sizes: 16×16 for browser tabs, 32×32 for desktop shortcuts, 48×48 for high-DPI displays, 180×180 as the Apple touch icon, and 192×192 for Android. Next, copy the pre-formatted HTML link snippet the tool provides and paste it into the head section of your website, so browsers know where to find each icon. Finally, click Download All (ZIP) to get every icon file packaged together in one archive. Unzip it, place the files with your site, and your branding shows up everywhere your site appears.

## Feature Deep Dive

The generator uses high-quality bicubic downscaling, which matters enormously at these sizes. Shrinking a logo to 16×16 pixels with crude scaling turns it into a blurry smudge; bicubic resampling keeps edges crisp and the shape recognisable even at microscopic dimensions. Each size in the set has a real job. The 16×16 icon is the one visitors see in their browser tabs; 32×32 serves desktop shortcuts and bookmarks; 48×48 covers high-DPI displays where a small icon would look fuzzy; 180×180 is the Apple touch icon shown when someone saves your site to an iPhone home screen; and 192×192 is the Android Chrome icon used for home-screen shortcuts and app-like installs. The tool also hands you the exact HTML link tags to include, so there is no guessing about filenames or paths. Because everything runs locally in your browser, your logo never leaves your device, and the icons are generated instantly with no queue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What makes a good favicon image?**

Simplicity. At 16 pixels square, fine detail, thin lines, and small text vanish, so a bold shape, a single letter, or a simplified mark works far better than a detailed illustration. High contrast between the mark and its background also helps it read clearly in a tab.

**Do I need a square image to start with?**

Yes, the generator is built for square graphics, so start with a square version of your logo. A wide or tall image will not frame well at these tiny sizes, and a square source keeps the icon centred and balanced in every size.

**Where do I put the favicon files on my site?**

Upload them alongside your website's files, in the location your HTML snippet points to. The link tags reference each file by name, so as long as the files sit where the snippet expects them, browsers will find and display them.

**Will the favicon show up immediately after I add it?**

Not always. Browsers cache favicons aggressively, so you may still see the old icon — or none at all — until the cache clears. A hard refresh usually fixes it, and new visitors will see the new icon right away.

**Can I use a photo as a favicon?**

You can, but photos rarely work well at these sizes. Faces and scenery turn into unrecognisable blobs at 16 pixels. A simple logo, icon, or initial will read far more clearly in a browser tab.
