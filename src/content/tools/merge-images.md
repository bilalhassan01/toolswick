## Why Client-Side Matters

A before-and-after pair, two halves of a panorama, or a set of photos that belong together often work best as a single image. Most merging tools upload your pictures to a server, where copies can be stored or scanned. This tool joins your images together entirely inside your browser, on a canvas in local memory. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and the combined picture downloads straight to your device.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Merging photos takes only a minute. First, upload two or more photos to combine by dragging them in or clicking to browse. Second, choose your arrangement: horizontal to place them side by side, vertical to stack them one above the other, or a 2 by 2 grid for four photos. Third, adjust the spacing between the photos and pick a background colour for the gaps and edges. The tool aligns the images automatically so widths or heights match cleanly. Finally, click "Download Merged Image" to export the combined high-resolution picture.

## Feature Deep Dive

The merger renders your photos onto a single canvas at full resolution, so the combined image stays sharp rather than being downscaled. Horizontal layouts are ideal for comparisons, because the eye moves naturally from one side to the other, while vertical stacking suits tall images such as screenshots, receipts, or phone photos that read top to bottom. The 2 by 2 grid packs four photos into one neat square, which works well for social posts. The spacing slider runs from 0 to 50 pixels: zero gives a seamless edge-to-edge join, while a wider gap with a chosen background colour creates a clean frame between the photos. Because the alignment is automatic, photos of different sizes line up without manual cropping, and your originals are never changed — the merged image is always a fresh download.

## Use Cases

Merged images are useful wherever two or more photos tell one story. The classic case is a before-and-after comparison — a room renovation, a haircut, a restored object — where placing the two shots side by side makes the change obvious at a glance. Sellers combine several angles of one product into a single listing image, and travellers join two halves of a view into one wide picture. It is also handy for documents: stacking two screenshots or two pages of a form into one image makes them easier to attach to a single email. Teachers and trainers merge diagram steps into one figure for a handout, and families combine photos from the same day into a single keepsake image.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do my photos have to be the same size to merge them?**

No. The tool aligns images automatically so their widths or heights match cleanly, even when the originals are different sizes.

**Which layout works best for a before-and-after comparison?**

Horizontal, side by side, is usually best — the eye compares the two images naturally. Use vertical stacking when the photos are tall, like phone screenshots.

**Can I merge more than four photos?**

The grid layout holds four photos, and the horizontal and vertical layouts can take more. For very large sets, merge them in groups and then merge the results together.

**Will the merged image keep its quality?**

Yes. The combined picture is rendered at full resolution on a canvas in your browser, so the photos stay as sharp as the originals.
