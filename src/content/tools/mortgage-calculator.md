## Why Client-Side Matters

A mortgage calculation involves the largest purchase most people ever make, and the figures are personal: the price of the home, the deposit you can put down, and the rate you have been offered. This tool works differently. Every figure is processed entirely inside your own browser, on your device, using the standard fixed-rate amortisation formula. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing remains behind after you close the tab. You can explore what a home would cost each month without telling anyone you are looking.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Working out a mortgage here takes four quick steps. First, enter the home purchase price and choose your currency symbol from the dropdown, which offers the dollar, euro, pound, yen, and rupee. Second, enter your down payment, either as a percentage of the price or as a fixed amount, using the toggle beside the box to switch between the two. Third, select the loan term from the dropdown, choosing thirty, twenty, fifteen, or ten years, and enter the annual interest rate as a percentage. Fourth, add the yearly costs: property tax per year, homeowners insurance per year, and any homeowners association fee per month. The results panel updates to show your total estimated monthly payment, broken down into principal and interest, the total loan principal, the total interest over the term, and the total amount repaid overall, followed by an annual amortisation schedule and a button to copy the quote.

## Feature Deep Dive

The monthly principal and interest payment is calculated with the standard fixed amortisation formula, which produces a level monthly payment for a fixed-rate loan. The loan principal is the purchase price minus the down payment, so a larger deposit directly reduces the amount borrowed. On top of principal and interest, the tool adds the monthly share of your yearly property tax and insurance, plus any monthly homeowners association fee, to give a total estimated monthly payment that reflects the real cost of owning the home, not just the loan itself.

The results panel keeps the headline figures in one place: the total estimated monthly payment, the principal and interest portion, the total loan principal, the total interest over the full term, and the total overall repaid. Below it, the annual amortisation schedule shows, for each year of the loan, how much principal was paid, how much interest was paid, and what balance remained at the end of the year.

## Use Cases

A mortgage calculator is useful at every stage of buying a home. First-time buyers use it to see what monthly payment different house prices would mean, and to test how much difference a larger down payment makes. Buyers comparing homes use it to check whether a more expensive house is actually affordable month to month once tax, insurance, and association fees are included. Anyone weighing a fifteen-year term against a thirty-year one can see the trade-off in plain numbers: higher monthly payments against far less total interest.

It is also useful for understanding a mortgage you already hold. The amortisation schedule shows how much of what you have paid so far went to interest and how much reduced the balance, and how the balance will fall in the years ahead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I enter the down payment as a dollar amount instead of a percentage?**

Yes. The toggle beside the down payment box switches between percentage and fixed amount. Enter twenty for twenty percent, or switch to the currency option and enter the exact deposit figure you have in mind.

**Which currencies can I use?**

The currency dropdown offers five symbols: dollar, euro, pound, yen, and rupee. The symbol changes the display only; the calculation itself works the same way whichever one you pick.

**Does the calculator include homeowners association fees in the monthly total?**

Yes. The HOA per month figure you enter is added to the total estimated monthly payment, alongside the monthly share of property tax and insurance and the principal and interest payment.

**Can I compare a fifteen-year term with a thirty-year term?**

Yes. Change the term in the dropdown and every result updates at once, including the monthly payment, the total interest, and the amortisation schedule, so you can see the trade-off side by side.

**Is this an official mortgage offer?**

No. The figures are estimates based on the numbers you entered, using the standard amortisation formula. A lender's actual offer will depend on their own terms and checks, so use the results for planning and confirm the final figures with your lender.
