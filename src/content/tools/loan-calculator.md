## Why Client-Side Matters

A loan calculation involves your money: how much you want to borrow, at what rate, and for how long. Many online calculators send those figures to a server, and some use the enquiry to collect contact details or show you offers. This tool works differently. Your loan figures are processed entirely inside your own browser, on your device, using the standard fixed-rate amortisation formula. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing remains behind after you close the tab. You can explore what borrowing would cost, at any amount or rate, without telling anyone you are looking.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Working out a loan here takes three quick steps. First, enter the loan amount in the Loan Amount box and choose your currency from the dropdown, which covers nine currencies including the US dollar, euro, British pound, Canadian and Australian dollars, Indian and Pakistani rupees, Saudi riyal, and UAE dirham. Second, enter the annual interest rate as a percentage, then set the loan term, choosing years or months to suit the kind of loan you are considering. Third, review the results. The tool shows your monthly payment, the total interest you would pay over the life of the loan, and the total amount repaid, followed by a year-by-year amortisation table breaking each year into principal and interest.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator uses the standard fixed-rate amortisation formula, the same approach lenders use to work out a level monthly payment on a fixed-rate loan. It takes the amount borrowed, the annual interest rate, and the term, and produces a monthly payment that stays the same throughout the loan, with the split between interest and principal shifting over time. Early payments go mostly towards interest, while later payments pay down more of the principal, which is why the amortisation table is so revealing: it shows, year by year, how much of each payment actually reduced what you owe.

The results cover the figures that matter when you are weighing a loan. The monthly payment tells you what the loan costs month to month. The total interest shows what borrowing costs on top of the amount itself, which is often the most surprising number on the page. The total repaid combines the two. And the amortisation table turns a single payment figure into a full picture of the loan's life, showing the balance falling year by year.

## Use Cases

A loan calculator is useful long before anyone signs anything. Home buyers use it to see what different house prices and rates would mean each month, and to compare a thirty-year term against a shorter one. Car buyers check what a vehicle loan costs at several terms before they visit a dealer. Anyone considering a personal loan uses it to test whether the monthly payment fits their budget, and to see how much extra a longer term really costs in total interest.

It is just as useful for understanding a loan you already have. The amortisation table shows how much of what you have paid so far went to interest and how much reduced the balance, which explains why the balance falls slowly at first. It also shows what an extra payment or a shorter term would do, in plain numbers. The results are estimates based on the figures you enter, not a loan offer: a lender's actual terms will depend on their own assessment, so treat the figures as a planning guide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use it for a car loan or a personal loan?**

Yes. The calculator uses the standard fixed-rate amortisation formula, which applies to mortgages, auto loans, and personal loans alike. Enter the amount, rate, and term for any of them and the same figures, monthly payment, total interest, and amortisation table, are shown.

**Does it include fees, taxes, or insurance?**

No. The calculation uses only the three figures you enter: the loan amount, the annual interest rate, and the term. Fees, taxes, and insurance are not part of this calculator; the companion mortgage tool covers property tax and insurance for home loans.

**What does the amortisation table show?**

It breaks the loan down year by year, showing how much of each year's payments went to interest, how much went to principal, and what balance remained at the end of the year, so you can follow the loan from start to finish.

**Can I compare different loan terms?**

Yes. Change the term, in years or months, and every result updates at once.

**Is the result a final loan offer?**

No. The figures are estimates based on the numbers you entered, using the standard amortisation formula. A lender's actual offer will depend on their own terms and checks, so use the results for planning and comparison.
