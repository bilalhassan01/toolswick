## Why Client-Side Matters

Turning a PDF into images is usually a job for a desktop program or an upload website, and the upload route is the problem. The PDF you want to convert might be a confidential report, a design proof, an invoice, or a personal document — sending it to a server means a copy exists somewhere you cannot control. This tool converts entirely inside your browser using Mozilla's open-source PDF.js engine. Your file is rendered onto HTML5 canvases on your own device, and the resulting images download straight to you. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and the conversion works the same on a slow connection as on a fast one once the page has loaded.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Converting a PDF to images takes three steps. First, drop your PDF into the upload zone, or click to browse and select it from your computer or phone. Second, choose your output format and resolution. Pick JPG for photographs and scanned pages, where a smaller file matters more than pixel-perfect edges, or PNG for charts, diagrams, and sharp line art, where lossless clarity is essential. Then pick a resolution: Standard at 96 DPI is fine for screen use, Crisp HD at 150 DPI looks sharper in presentations, and Ultra HD at 300 DPI is print-ready. Third, click the convert button. Every page is rendered into its own image, which you can preview before downloading. Download pages individually, or grab them all at once as a single ZIP archive.

## Feature Deep Dive

The conversion engine is PDF.js, the same open-source renderer used by Firefox. It reads the PDF's vector fonts and graphics and draws them onto HTML5 canvases with pixel-perfect fidelity, so typography stays crisp and colours reproduce accurately. Because the source is vector data rather than a screenshot, text edges stay sharp even at 300 DPI, and diagrams keep their clean lines.

Every page in the document is converted — not just the first one — and each becomes a separate image file. That makes the tool practical for whole documents, not just single pages. The two output formats cover the common needs: JPG produces smaller files and suits photographs and scanned documents, while PNG is lossless and suits anything with text, lines, or flat colour. The three resolution levels map to real uses: 96 DPI for web and messaging, 150 DPI for slides and documents, and 300 DPI for print. All of the rendering happens on your device, so multi-page documents convert without an upload queue or a waiting room.The images you download are standard JPG and PNG files, ready to use anywhere — in a document, on a web page, in a chat, or in an editor. Converting does not alter your original PDF in any way; the file stays exactly as it was, and you can convert it again at a different resolution or format whenever you like. One practical tip is to match the resolution to the destination: there is no benefit in converting at 300 DPI for a chat message, where the image will be shown small, just as there is no substitute for 300 DPI when the image is going to print. Choosing the right resolution keeps file sizes sensible while preserving the quality you actually need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I convert a PDF with mixed portrait and landscape pages?**

Yes. Each page is rendered as its own image at its own size and orientation, so a document mixing portrait and landscape pages converts cleanly, page by page.

**Will the converted images keep the PDF's colours?**

Yes. The renderer reproduces colours accurately, so charts, photographs, and branding keep their intended appearance in the converted images.

**What resolution should I choose?**

Standard (96 DPI) is fine for screens and messaging. Crisp HD (150 DPI) looks sharper in presentations and documents. Ultra HD (300 DPI) is print-ready quality.

**Can I use the converted images on a website?**

Yes. They are standard JPG and PNG files, ready to embed in a web page, a document, a chat message, or an editor — no special software needed to view them.

**Can I convert just one page instead of the whole document?**

Yes. Every page is converted into its own image, and you can download pages individually. Convert the full document, then download only the page you need.
