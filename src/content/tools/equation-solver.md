## Why Client-Side Matters

Solving equations is homework, revision, or engineering work — and it does not need a server to get involved. Every step of the algebra here runs inside your browser, so the coefficients you type never leave your device, are never stored, and disappear when you close the tab. There is no account and no upload, just the answer and the working, kept entirely private.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Choose your equation type first: Quadratic, Linear, or a 2×2 System of two linear equations. For a quadratic, enter the three coefficients — the numbers in front of x², x, and the constant term. For a linear equation, enter its two coefficients. For a system, enter the coefficients of both equations. The solution appears at once: the roots and the discriminant for a quadratic, or the values of x and y for a system. Below the answer, the tool shows the complete step-by-step derivation, so you can follow exactly how it got there.

## Feature Deep Dive

The quadratic solver uses the quadratic formula to find the roots of any second-degree equation, and it also shows the vertex — the turning point of the parabola — and the discriminant, the part under the square root that decides what kind of roots exist. A positive discriminant means two real roots, zero means one repeated root, and a negative discriminant means complex roots, which the tool gives exactly, with i, rather than failing. Systems of two equations are solved with Cramer's rule, using determinants to find x and y directly. The step-by-step working is the most useful part for learners: it shows each stage of the algebra instead of only the final answer. A linear equation, meanwhile, is solved in a single step, but the tool still shows the working so you can see the rearrangement plainly.

## Use Cases

Students check their homework and, more importantly, see where their own working went wrong by comparing it with the tool's steps. Exam candidates revise quadratics by testing themselves on the steps, then using the solver to confirm. Teachers generate worked examples quickly for a lesson or a worksheet. Engineers and technicians solving two simultaneous equations — for example, balancing currents or forces — get an exact answer without setting up a spreadsheet. Anyone who has forgotten the quadratic formula gets both the answer and a reminder of how it works.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does the discriminant tell me?**

It tells you what kind of roots a quadratic has before you finish solving it. A positive discriminant means two different real roots, zero means one repeated root, and a negative discriminant means the roots are complex numbers involving i.

**What is the vertex of a quadratic?**

It is the turning point of the parabola — the lowest point if the curve opens upwards, or the highest point if it opens downwards. The solver shows it alongside the roots, which helps when sketching the graph.

**What happens if a system of equations has no solution?**

When the two lines are parallel, the determinant is zero and there is no single point where they meet. The solver reports this instead of inventing an answer, so you know the system has no unique solution.

**Can the solver handle equations with fractions as coefficients?**

Yes. Enter coefficients as decimals and the tool solves them the same way. For exact fraction answers, convert the decimals back to fractions from the step-by-step working shown.
