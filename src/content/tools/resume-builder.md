## Why Client-Side Matters

A resume contains the most sensitive facts about your working life: your phone number, email, address, job history, and education. Uploading that to a resume website means trusting a company you have never met with your identity, and many of those sites keep your data, sell it, or charge you to download your own document. This tool builds your resume entirely in your browser. Your personal and career details never leave your device, never enter a database, and the finished resume is exported by you, on your terms, as a PDF you own.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start with your contact information and a short executive career summary — two or three lines about who you are and what you do best. Next, add your work history, with a position, employer, dates, and the achievements that matter for each role; use the + Add Position button for every job you want to include. Then add your education with the + Add Degree button, and list your core skills as tags. A live preview shows the resume taking shape as you type, so you can see the layout and fix anything that looks off. When it is ready, click Print / Save Resume as PDF and choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialogue to export the finished file.

## Feature Deep Dive

The builder is structured around what hiring managers and applicant tracking systems actually read. The layout is clean and linear, with no complex graphics, tables, or columns that confuse parsing software, so your text is read accurately by ATS filters. Typography and spacing follow a simple hierarchy: your name and summary first, then work history, education, and skills, each in its own clear section. Work history and education sections are expandable — add as many positions and degrees as your career needs — and skills are entered as tags, which keeps the list tidy and scannable. The real-time preview means you are never guessing how the page will look, and the print-to-PDF export produces a finished document ready to attach to any application.

## Use Cases

First-time job seekers use it to turn a blank page into a proper resume in under an hour. Experienced workers build a master resume, then make a tailored copy for each application by editing the summary and reordering achievements. Career changers use it to reframe their history around transferable skills. Freelancers and consultants produce a one-page professional summary for proposals and client introductions. It also works for a quick refresh: update one job or add a new skill and export a current version in minutes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is my resume saved anywhere?**

No. Everything you type stays in your browser and is never uploaded or stored. Export the PDF before you close the tab, because once the page is gone, so is the draft.

**Can I make different versions for different jobs?**

Yes. Build your main resume, export it, then edit the summary and work history for the next role and export again. Each version is a separate PDF, so you can keep a tailored resume for every application.

**What should go in the career summary?**

Two or three lines that say what you do, how long you have done it, and your strongest result. For example, a role, years of experience, and one measurable achievement give a hiring manager a reason to keep reading.

**How long should my resume be?**

One page is right for most early-career applicants, and two pages is acceptable once you have a decade or more of relevant experience. If it runs longer, cut the oldest roles to a single line each and keep the focus on recent work.
