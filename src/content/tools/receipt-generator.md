## Why Client-Side Matters

A receipt is a small document with a surprising amount of private detail in it: your business name and address, what was sold, what it cost, and how it was paid for. Sending that to a server just to format it is an unnecessary exposure, and keeping receipts in someone else's system is worse. This tool builds the receipt entirely in your browser. Your business details and sales records never leave your device, never sit in a database you do not control, and disappear when you close the tab unless you choose to print or save them yourself.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start by entering your business details: the shop name, address, telephone number, and the name of the clerk serving the customer. Next, add the items one by one, with a quantity and a price for each — the tool keeps a running subtotal as you go. Choose the payment method: cash, which works out the change due from the amount tendered, or card, where you can record the last four digits. You can also edit the header and footer lines, such as a thank-you message or a returns note. When the receipt looks right, click Print Thermal Receipt and use your printer, or save it as a PDF from the print dialogue.

## Feature Deep Dive

The layout copies a real thermal POS receipt: a narrow 80mm format, monospace type, and dashed dividers between sections, so the result looks like it came from a shop till rather than a word processor. Tax is calculated on the itemised subtotal, and the totals section shows the subtotal, tax, and final amount clearly. Cash payments include a change calculation from the amount the customer hands over, while card payments record the last four digits for reference. Every line of the header, store location, and footer greeting is editable, so the receipt carries your wording, not a template's. The print output is sized for standard 80mm thermal roll printers and also works on an ordinary desktop printer.

## Use Cases

Market stall holders and pop-up sellers use it to give customers a proper receipt without buying a till system. Small shops use it as a backup when their POS is down, or for sales made away from the counter. Freelancers and tradespeople issue receipts for cash jobs, keeping a clean paper trail for their accounts. It also suits charity sales, school fairs, and community events, where a simple itemised receipt adds trust. Anyone who needs a record of a sale — for tax, for expenses, or for a customer's peace of mind — can produce one in a minute.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I save a receipt as a PDF instead of printing?**

Yes. In the print dialogue, choose "Save as PDF" as the destination instead of a printer. You get a clean PDF copy of the receipt to email to the customer or file with your records.

**Does it calculate tax automatically?**

Yes. Tax is worked out from your itemised subtotal and shown as its own line, with the final total underneath. You see the full breakdown before you print, so there are no surprises at the till.

**Can I reprint a receipt later?**

Not from the tool, because receipts are not stored anywhere — that is the privacy trade-off. Print the receipt or save it as a PDF while the page is still open, and keep that copy for your records.

**What paper size should I use?**

The layout is designed for 80mm thermal roll paper, the standard for shop receipt printers. On a desktop printer it scales to a normal page, so you can print a copy on A4 or letter just as easily.
