## Why Client-Side Matters

A game night without dice is a game night that never starts — and physical dice get lost, roll under the sofa, or simply are not in the bag when you need them. Many dice apps are cluttered with ads or ask for permissions they do not need. This tool rolls virtual dice entirely in your browser, from a D4 to a D100, with totals, modifiers, and a roll history. Nothing is installed, nothing is uploaded, and the dice are ready the moment the page loads.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Rolling dice takes seconds. First, select your die type: D4, D6, D8, D10, D12, D20, or D100. Second, choose how many dice to roll at once, from 1 to 10. Third, add an optional modifier to the final sum — a plus or minus that many tabletop games add to a roll. Finally, click "Roll Dice" or press the Spacebar to roll, and the tool shows each individual die result alongside the total sum.

## Feature Deep Dive

The roller covers the full set of tabletop dice, so it works for board games that need a standard D6, role-playing games built around the D20, and percentile rolls that need a D100. Rolling up to ten dice at once with an instant cumulative total saves the slow work of adding up a handful of dice by hand, and the modifier field handles the bonuses and penalties that most RPG rules apply to a roll. Every roll is generated with cryptographically secure random values, so the results are fair and unpredictable — no weighted dice, no patterns, and no way to predict the next roll. The roll history keeps a record of recent results, which settles table arguments about what was rolled last, and the animation gives each roll a moment of drama before the numbers appear.

## Use Cases

Virtual dice save game night in more ways than one. Role-playing groups use them for D20 attack rolls, damage rolls, and skill checks, with modifiers applied automatically. Board game players use them when the physical dice are missing or when a game needs an unusual die type. Teachers use them in maths lessons to demonstrate probability and chance, letting a class roll a hundred times and watch the results even out. They are also handy for quick decisions and party games — roll a D6 to pick between options, or a D20 to settle a friendly contest — and for solo players who want a random result without carrying a bag of dice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does D20 mean?**

It means a die with 20 sides, numbered 1 to 20. D6 is the familiar six-sided die, D4 has four sides, and D100 is a percentile die that gives a result from 1 to 100.

**Can I roll different types of dice together?**

The tool rolls one type at a time, up to ten dice of that type. For a mixed roll, roll each type separately and add the results together.

**How does the modifier work?**

The modifier is added to or subtracted from the total after the dice are rolled. A +2 modifier, for example, adds 2 to the sum of the dice, just like a skill bonus in a role-playing game.

**Are the rolls really random?**

Yes. The results come from your browser's cryptographically secure random generator, so every face of the die has an equal chance on every roll.
