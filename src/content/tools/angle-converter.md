## Why Client-Side Matters

Angles and frequencies turn up in homework, engineering sketches, and workshop settings, and none of that needs to leave your device. A conversion is pure arithmetic: degrees to radians, RPM to hertz, and back again. This tool does the whole calculation inside your browser, so the numbers you type never travel to a server, are never logged, and disappear when you close the tab. There is nothing to install and no account involved, just a quick answer that stays private.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start by choosing the conversion type at the top of the page: Plane Angle for geometric angles, or Frequency for rotational speed. Type your value into the entry box — the tool starts with 180 as an example. Pick the unit you are converting from and the unit you want, using the swap button if you get them backwards. The converted value appears at once, together with the formula used, so you can see exactly how the answer was reached. Below that, a table shows the same value in every unit at once, which is handy when you need more than one conversion. A copy button puts the result on your clipboard.

## Feature Deep Dive

The plane-angle mode covers degrees, radians, gradians, and full turns. A full circle is 360 degrees, 2π radians, or 400 gradians, and the tool converts between them using exact mathematical constants, so 180 degrees comes out as exactly π radians. The frequency mode covers hertz, kilohertz, megahertz, and revolutions per minute, converting rotational speed into cycles per second and back: RPM divided by 60 gives hertz. The formula line under the result shows the exact relationship used, which makes the tool useful for checking homework as well as getting an answer. Results update live as you type, so changing a digit updates every figure on the page instantly.

## Use Cases

Students use the converter for trigonometry and physics homework, switching between degrees and radians without reaching for a calculator. Engineers and machinists convert rotational speeds when a motor is rated in RPM but a specification calls for hertz. Hobbyists working with stepper motors, turntables, or 3D printers translate between turns and degrees when setting rotation angles. Surveyors and navigators, who sometimes work in gradians, convert to degrees to match maps and instruments. Anyone comparing datasheets from different countries uses the all-units table to see every equivalent at a glance instead of converting one pair at a time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I convert an angle larger than a full circle?**

Yes. The tool accepts any number, including values above 360 degrees or 2π radians, and converts them directly without wrapping them back to a single circle. If you need the equivalent within one turn, subtract full turns from the result yourself.

**What is the difference between a turn and a rotation?**

Nothing — they are two names for the same thing: one complete circle. One turn equals 360 degrees, 2π radians, or 400 gradians, and the tool treats the terms as identical.

**Why does the tool show a formula under the result?**

So you can check the maths. The formula line shows the exact relationship used, such as multiplying degrees by π/180 to get radians, which helps when you are learning or double-checking a result.

**Does the converter work offline?**

Once the page has loaded, the calculations run entirely in your browser with no server involved, so the conversion itself needs no connection afterwards.
