## Why Client-Side Matters

Fraction sums come up in homework, recipes, and workshop measurements — small, everyday arithmetic that has no business leaving your device. This tool does all the calculation inside your browser, so the numbers you type never travel to a server, are never stored, and vanish when you close the tab. There is no account and no upload, just the answer and the working, kept completely private.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Enter the numerator and denominator of the first fraction, then choose your operation: add, subtract, multiply, or divide. Enter the second fraction the same way. The result appears immediately, shown as a simplified fraction, as a mixed number where that applies, and as a decimal value. Below the answer, the tool shows the step-by-step breakdown, including the common denominator used and how the result was reduced. Change any number and the whole answer updates at once.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator handles all four operations on fractions and mixed numbers. For addition and subtraction it finds a common denominator, converts both fractions, then combines the numerators. For multiplication it multiplies straight across; for division it flips the second fraction and multiplies. Every result is simplified automatically using the greatest common divisor, so an answer like 4/8 is reduced to 1/2 without you having to ask. The step breakdown shows each intermediate stage — the common denominator, the converted fractions, and the reduction — which is what makes it useful for learning, not just for getting an answer. Mixed numbers are handled too: a result larger than one is shown as a mixed number as well as an improper fraction, and the decimal value sits beside both so you can check the answer in whichever form you trust most.

## Use Cases

Students check fraction homework and use the steps to see where their own working went wrong. Cooks scale a recipe up or down, adding or halving fractional cup measurements without guessing. Woodworkers and carpenters add and subtract fractional inch measurements when cutting and joining. Sewists and crafters work out fabric lengths in fractions of a yard or metre. Anyone splitting a bill, a measurement, or a share into fractions gets an exact answer instead of a rounded decimal that drifts. Bakers and home cooks scaling a recipe for a different tin size rely on the same thing: the fractions stay exact through every step, so the proportions never quietly go wrong.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a mixed number, and when does the answer use one?**

A mixed number combines a whole number with a fraction, like 1½. When a result is larger than one, the tool shows it this way as well as an improper fraction, so you can read it in whichever form suits the task.

**How do I enter a mixed number as an input?**

Convert it to an improper fraction first: multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator. For example, 2¾ becomes 11/4 — enter 11 over 4.

**Why does the tool simplify the answer automatically?**

Because a simplified fraction is the standard finished form in maths and in practical work. Showing 2/3 instead of 8/12 makes the answer easier to read, measure, and use in the next step.

**What happens if I enter zero as a denominator?**

The tool cannot divide by zero, so it will not produce an answer for that fraction. Enter a non-zero denominator — a fraction with zero on the bottom has no defined value.
