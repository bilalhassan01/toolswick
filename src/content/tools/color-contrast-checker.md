## Why Client-Side Matters

Text that is hard to read excludes people. Low-vision readers, older readers, and anyone reading in bright sunlight all struggle when text and background colours are too close together, and many websites still get this wrong. Checking contrast is a quick fix, and it should not cost you your privacy. This checker runs entirely in your browser: you pick two colours, the ratio is calculated on your device, and nothing about your design is sent to a server. You can test a whole palette in minutes without uploading a thing.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Checking a colour pair takes seconds. First, choose your text colour with the colour picker or type a hex value directly. Second, choose the background colour the same way. A live preview shows sample text in your exact combination, so you can judge it with your own eyes as well as the numbers. Third, read the contrast ratio and the pass or fail marks for WCAG AA and AAA, for both normal and large text. If a pair fails, adjust one colour slightly — often darkening the text a little is enough — until the marks turn green.

## Feature Deep Dive

The checker measures the contrast ratio between two colours, the same number the WCAG 2.1 accessibility standard uses. WCAG AA asks for at least 4.5 to 1 for normal body text and 3 to 1 for large text, which is roughly 18 point or 14 point bold and above. WCAG AAA is stricter, asking for 7 to 1 for normal text and 4.5 to 1 for large text. The tool shows all four results at once, so you can see whether a pair passes the everyday AA bar or the tougher AAA bar. A swap button flips foreground and background instantly, which is handy when you are testing a dark-mode version of the same design. The live preview uses a full sentence of sample text, so you can see how body copy actually reads in the combination, not just a coloured square.

## Use Cases

Web designers check body text, buttons, and links against page backgrounds before a site goes live. App developers test light and dark themes to make sure both stay readable. Brand teams check whether a logo colour works as text on a website or slide deck. Bloggers and content creators verify that coloured headings and callout boxes are readable. Teachers and public-sector teams check documents and presentations for accessibility. Anyone who has ever squinted at pale grey text on a white page knows why this check matters — it is the difference between a design that looks good and one that can actually be read.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between normal text and large text?**

Large text means roughly 18 point or larger, or 14 point bold and larger. It needs less contrast because thicker, bigger letters are easier to read, so the AA bar drops from 4.5 to 1 down to 3 to 1.

**My colours pass AA but fail AAA — is that a problem?**

Not usually. AA is the standard most websites aim for, and it is the level referenced by most accessibility laws and guidelines. AAA is an enhanced target for specialist content, such as material aimed at low-vision readers.

**Can I check more than one colour pair at a time?**

The checker tests one pair at a time, but each check takes seconds. Work through your palette pair by pair — body text, headings, buttons, and links — and note any that fail.

**Does a passing ratio guarantee my text is readable?**

It guarantees the contrast meets the standard, which is the biggest factor. Font choice, size, and spacing still matter, so always look at the live preview and, when you can, ask a real person to read it.
