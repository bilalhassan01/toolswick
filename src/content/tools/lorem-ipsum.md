## Why Client-Side Matters

Designers and developers constantly need text that looks real but means nothing yet: a page layout, a font test, or a client mockup where the final words have not been written. Typing filler by hand wastes time and produces uneven blocks, while copying it from a random website means trusting a page you do not need. This tool generates the placeholder text right in your browser, instantly, with no upload and no account. The words are drawn from a classic Latin word list and assembled locally, so you can produce exactly the amount you need — a few words, a few sentences, or several paragraphs — and paste it straight into your layout. Nothing leaves your device, and nothing interrupts the work.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Generating placeholder text takes seconds. First, choose what you need: paragraphs, sentences, or an exact word count. Paragraphs suit full page mockups, sentences suit short blocks and captions, and word counts suit tight spaces where the length has to be precise. Second, enter the quantity you require, from 1 to 50. Third, click Generate Lorem Ipsum and the text appears, ready to copy. Each click produces a fresh mix, because the words are drawn at random from the word list, so generating twice gives you a different passage to work with. Paste the result into your design, document, or mockup, and repeat whenever a new block needs filling. There is no waiting, no download, and no formatting to strip out afterwards.

## Feature Deep Dive

The generator builds its text from a word list of classic Latin terms — lorem, ipsum, dolor, sit, amet, and dozens more — picking words at random and assembling them into natural-looking sentences. Each sentence runs between 8 and 18 words, starts with a capital letter, and ends with a full stop, which gives the passage the rhythm and shape of real prose. A paragraph is made of four to seven such sentences, so a paragraph block reads like a genuine chunk of body copy rather than a repeated line. Because the words are random, no two generations are identical, and the text never settles into a visible repeating pattern across a long page. That matters for layout work: real text has varied word lengths and a natural distribution of letters, and this generator reproduces that texture, so spacing, line breaks, and column widths in your mockup behave the way they will with the final copy.

## Use Cases

Web designers use placeholder text to show a client what a page will look like before the real copy exists — a homepage, a blog layout, or a landing page filled with realistic blocks instead of empty grey boxes. Print designers test brochures, magazines, and flyers, checking how columns flow and where headings fall. Developers building templates and content systems use it to fill demo pages, so a theme can be judged on its typography rather than its emptiness. It is also the standard tool for font and typesetting tests: a paragraph of Lorem Ipsum shows how a typeface handles long words, punctuation, and line spacing far better than a repeated sample sentence. And when a wireframe needs to feel like a finished page in a presentation, a few paragraphs of placeholder text make it look complete without anyone having to write a word of real copy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does it always start with "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet"?**

No. The words are picked at random from the Latin word list, so each generation is different. The opening words vary every time you click generate.

**How much placeholder text should I generate?**

Match the length of the real content that will replace it. One or two paragraphs per text block is usually enough to judge spacing, line breaks, and column widths.

**Why use Latin instead of real English words?**

Because readable English distracts the reader from the layout. Latin-looking text lets reviewers focus on the design, spacing, and typography instead of reading — and arguing about — the words.

**Can I generate an exact number of words?**

Yes. Choose the word-count mode and enter any quantity from 1 to 50, and the generator will produce exactly that many words.

**Will the text ever repeat the same paragraph twice?**

Each click draws fresh random words, so the output varies every time. Two generations may share individual words, but whole identical paragraphs are very unlikely.
