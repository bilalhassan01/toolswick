## Why Client-Side Matters

A folder of full-size phone photos can easily run into hundreds of megabytes, which makes it slow to attach to emails, painful to upload to a website, and wasteful of storage. Most batch compressors ask you to send that whole folder to a server, where your private pictures sit on someone else's machine. This tool compresses every image inside your own browser instead. Your photos never leave your device, nothing is uploaded, and nothing is stored anywhere else — the smaller files are produced locally and downloaded straight to you.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Compressing a batch of photos takes only a few minutes. First, drop all the photos you want to compress into the batch zone, or click to select them from your device — you can mix JPG and PNG files in one batch. Second, set your controls: a target maximum file size in kilobytes and a maximum dimension, so very large photos are scaled down as well as compressed. The tool shows the total size before and after, so you can see exactly how much space you are saving. Third, click "Compress All Photos" and wait for the batch to finish. Finally, click "Download All as ZIP" to save every compressed image in a single tidy archive.

## Feature Deep Dive

The compressor works on each image in turn, re-encoding it at the quality level you choose while keeping the visible detail as close to the original as possible. Photos typically shrink by 50 to 80 percent with barely any visible change, because most of the data being removed is detail the eye cannot easily see. The maximum-dimension control is just as important as the quality setting: a 4000-pixel-wide phone photo destined for a web page or an email does not need all those pixels, and capping the dimensions can cut the file size dramatically on its own. Mixed formats are handled in one pass, so a folder of JPGs and PNGs can be processed together without sorting them first. Everything runs in local JavaScript, so there is no server queue, no waiting for uploads, and no limit imposed by a remote service — the only practical limit is your browser's memory.

Batch compression is a lifesaver for anyone who handles lots of photos at once. Website owners shrink whole galleries before uploading them, which makes pages load faster and keeps visitors from bouncing away. Photographers and estate agents prepare client galleries and property listings without emailing enormous attachments. Students and office workers compress folders of scanned documents and screenshots so they fit under email attachment limits. Families back up holiday photos to smaller files to free up phone and cloud storage. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will my compressed photos look noticeably worse?**

In most cases, no. Photos typically shrink by 50 to 80 percent with minimal visible change, because the compression removes fine detail that is hard to see. If you are working with images that will be printed large, keep the quality setting higher.

**What happens to very large photos in the batch?**

The maximum-dimension setting scales them down as well as compressing them, so a huge phone photo is resized to a sensible width before it is re-encoded. That combination is what produces the biggest savings.

**Do I have to compress one photo at a time?**

No. You can drop a whole folder of photos into the batch zone at once and compress them all in a single run, then download everything together as one ZIP file.

**Are my photos safe while they are being compressed?**

Yes. Every image is processed inside your browser on your own device. Nothing is uploaded to a server, and no copy of your photos is stored anywhere.
