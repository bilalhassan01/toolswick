## Why Client-Side Matters

Pay is personal. Converting an hourly rate into an annual salary, or working out what a new job offer is really worth, should not mean typing your wages into a website that stores them. This tool does every calculation inside your own browser, on your device. Nothing you enter is uploaded, stored, or shared, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. You can compare offers and check your pay in complete privacy.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Choose whether to start from an hourly wage or an annual salary. Enter your rate, then enter the hours you typically work each week. If your schedule differs from the standard, adjust the days worked per week and the weeks worked per year. The tool then shows your pay broken down by hour, week, month, and year, along with your expected paycheck for each pay period. If you work overtime, enter the extra hours and the overtime multiplier, and the tool adds the gross overtime earnings to the result. Change any figure and every breakdown updates instantly.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator is built on the standard full-time assumption of 40 hours a week across 52 weeks, which totals 2,080 working hours a year, and it adjusts that figure when you enter different hours or weeks. Pay periods are broken down exactly: a bi-weekly schedule divides the annual salary into 26 paychecks, while a semi-monthly schedule divides it into 24, so the two look different even though the yearly total is the same. The overtime section multiplies your extra hours by the multiplier you set, which is typically one and a half times your normal rate, and adds it to your gross earnings. Because every breakdown is shown together, you can see at a glance what an hourly rate means as a monthly budget, or what an annual salary means as an hourly figure.

## Use Cases

Job seekers use the calculator to compare offers, turning an hourly rate from one employer into an annual figure they can set beside a salary from another. Freelancers and contractors use it to set their rates, checking what an hourly charge adds up to over a year. Employees use it to check a payslip, work out what a raise is worth per month, or see how overtime changes their take-home picture. Managers use it when budgeting for a new hire, and anyone negotiating pay uses it to translate between hourly, weekly, and annual figures so both sides are talking about the same number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the calculator deduct tax?**

No. The figures are gross pay, before tax and other deductions. Tax depends on where you live and your personal circumstances, so the tool shows the full amount and leaves tax to your payslip or a tax calculator.

**What if I work part-time or irregular hours?**

Enter your actual average hours per week and the number of weeks you work each year. The calculator adjusts the annual total from those figures instead of assuming a full-time schedule, so part-time and seasonal work are covered too.

**Can I compare two different job offers?**

Yes. Run the first offer through the calculator and note the annual and monthly figures, then change the rate and hours to the second offer. Comparing the same breakdowns side by side makes the better offer clear.

**Is my pay information kept private?**

Yes. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser. Your rates and hours are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
