## Why Client-Side Matters

Reading a long article, proofreading your own writing, or learning how a word is pronounced is easier when you can hear the text spoken aloud. Many text-to-speech services send your text to a server, which is a problem for private documents, drafts, or personal messages. This tool speaks your text using the speech synthesis built into your own browser and operating system. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and your words never leave your device.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Listening to text takes seconds. First, paste or type your text into the reader box — an article, an essay, a script, or a few sentences you want to hear pronounced. Second, select a voice from the dropdown list; the tool loads every voice installed on your operating system, so the choices depend on your device. Third, adjust the speed rate and pitch sliders until the voice sounds natural to you. Finally, click "Play Speech" to listen, and use "Pause" and "Stop" to control playback whenever you need to.

## Feature Deep Dive

The tool is powered by your browser's native SpeechSynthesis, which means the voices come from your own device rather than a remote service — that is why there is no lag, no upload, and no limit on how much text you can listen to. The speed control runs from 0.5 times slow to 2.0 times fast, so you can slow the voice down to catch every word in a language you are learning, or speed it up to skim through a long article. The pitch slider changes how high or low the voice sounds, which helps when a default voice feels too sharp or too flat. Pause and resume let you stop mid-sentence to take notes or think, and because the voices are the ones installed on your system, the same text can sound quite different on a phone, a laptop, or a tablet.

## Use Cases

Text to speech is useful for more than convenience. Writers listen to their own essays and stories to catch awkward sentences, repeated words, and mistakes the eye skips over — hearing your writing is one of the best proofreading tricks there is. Language learners hear correct pronunciation and rhythm by listening to sentences they have written. Students listen to study notes while walking or doing chores, turning dead time into revision time. It also helps people with tired eyes or reading difficulties, who can listen to an article instead of staring at a screen, and anyone who wants to check how a speech or a presentation sounds before delivering it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do the voices look different on my phone and my computer?**

The tool uses the voices installed on each device's operating system, so the list depends on your device. A phone, a laptop, and a tablet each have their own set of voices.

**Can I listen to a very long article in one go?**

Yes. There is no server limit, because everything runs locally. For very long texts, listen in sections so you can pause and take notes.

**What speed should I use for proofreading?**

Slightly slower than normal — around 0.8 times — gives your ear time to catch mistakes. For skimming a long article, try 1.5 times or faster.

**Can I use this to check how my writing sounds out loud?**

Yes, and it is one of the best uses. Awkward phrasing, repeated words, and missing words are much easier to hear than to see on the page.
