## Why Client-Side Matters

Zakat involves your savings, your gold, your investments, and your debts, and that is financial information that should never travel to a server. This tool calculates your Zakat entirely inside your own browser, on your device. Nothing you enter is uploaded, stored, or shared, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. You can work out what you owe in complete privacy.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start by entering your cash savings, both in bank accounts and cash kept at home. Then enter the value of your gold and silver, along with your investments and business stock. Next, enter any immediate short-term liabilities, such as bills due or debts payable soon. The tool adds up your Zakatable wealth, subtracts what you owe, and shows your net Zakatable wealth with the exact 2.5 percent Zakat payable on it. Change any figure and the result updates instantly, so you can see how each asset or debt affects the final amount.

## Feature Deep Dive

Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam, and the standard rate on qualifying wealth is 2.5 percent per lunar year. The calculator follows that rule: it totals your cash, gold, silver, investments, and business stock, then deducts your immediate debts and living expenses to arrive at your net Zakatable wealth. The 2.5 percent is applied to that net figure, so you only pay on what you truly hold after what you owe. The tool also works with the concept of Nisab, the minimum threshold of wealth above which Zakat becomes due, equivalent to 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver. If your net wealth is below that threshold, no Zakat is due on it.

## Use Cases

Muslims use the calculator each year, usually during Ramadan, to work out exactly how much Zakat they owe before giving it. It helps people with mixed assets, cash, gold, shares, and business stock, to bring everything together into one figure instead of calculating each part separately. Business owners use it to include their stock and trading goods alongside personal savings, and families use it to check whether their combined wealth reaches the Nisab threshold. It is also useful for anyone new to calculating Zakat, since it lays out each step clearly and shows how the final amount is reached.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which assets count towards Zakat?**

Cash savings, gold and silver, investments such as shares, and business stock and trading goods generally count as Zakatable wealth. Personal items you use every day, such as your home and your car, are not counted. If you are unsure about a specific asset, ask a trusted scholar.

**When during the year should I pay Zakat?**

Zakat is due once a lunar year has passed on wealth above the Nisab threshold. Many people choose Ramadan for its blessings, but any time after your Zakat year completes is acceptable, as long as it is paid once the year has passed.

**Do I subtract all my debts, or only short-term ones?**

Only immediate, short-term liabilities are deducted, such as bills due now or debts payable in the near term. Long-term debts are usually handled differently, so if you have a mortgage or a long-term loan, ask a scholar how to treat it.

**Is my financial information kept private?**

Yes. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser. Your savings, assets, and debts are never uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
