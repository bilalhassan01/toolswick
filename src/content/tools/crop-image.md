## Why Client-Side Matters

Cropping is usually a quick, personal edit: trimming a family photo, cutting a screenshot down to the relevant part, or framing a profile picture. Doing it on an upload website means sending a private image to a server first, where a copy can sit in storage even after you have finished. This tool crops entirely inside your browser, in local canvas memory. Your image never leaves your computer or phone, so a personal photo stays personal. There is no account, no upload queue, and nothing left behind when you close the tab — the crop happens where the image already is.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Cropping an image here is a hands-on process. First, drop your image into the tool, or browse to select a photo from your computer or phone. Second, adjust the crop area. Drag the rectangle to move it, or click one of the aspect ratio presets — 1:1 for a square profile picture, 16:9 for a wide banner, 4:3 for a classic photo frame, or 9:16 for a vertical story format. Locking a ratio keeps the proportions fixed while you resize. Third, fine-tune the result with the corner handles, pulling them in or out until the framing is exactly right. When you are happy, click the crop and download button to export the result as PNG or JPG. You can crop the same image several different ways in one session — a square version for a profile and a wide version for a banner, for example.

## Feature Deep Dive

The crop tool works directly on the image's pixels in local canvas memory, which is why it is both fast and precise. The draggable rectangle shows you exactly what will be kept, and the corner handles let you adjust each edge independently. When you lock an aspect ratio, the rectangle keeps those proportions no matter how you resize it, so a 1:1 crop stays perfectly square and a 16:9 crop stays exactly widescreen. That matters when a platform demands a specific shape: a square avatar, a widescreen cover, or a vertical story.

Quality is preserved by design. The cropped region keeps 100 percent of the original pixel fidelity, and exporting as PNG avoids any re-compression, so the result is as sharp as the source. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I crop a screenshot?**

Yes. Drop any image, including a screenshot, and crop out the parts you do not want — browser chrome, notifications, or anything else — before sharing it.

**What aspect ratio should I use for a profile picture?**

Use 1:1 (square) for most profile pictures. It is the shape nearly every platform expects for an avatar, and it keeps the subject centred.

**What formats can I export?**

You can download the cropped image as PNG or JPG. Choose PNG for maximum quality and sharpness, or JPG for a smaller file size.

**Can I undo a crop?**

The original image is never modified, so there is nothing to undo — just crop the source again with a different framing. The downloaded file is a new copy.

**Can I crop the same image more than once?**

Yes. The original is never modified, so you can crop it as many times as you like — a square version for a profile picture and a wide version for a banner, for example — without losing the source image.
