## Why Client-Side Matters

Pulling email addresses and links out of a long document, a log file, or a page of raw code by hand is slow, boring, and easy to get wrong — one missed address can mean a lost contact. Many extraction tools ask you to paste your text onto a server, which is a bad idea when that text contains private emails or internal links. This tool runs its extraction entirely inside your browser. Your text never leaves your device, nothing is saved or transmitted, and the results are ready in seconds.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Extracting addresses takes less than a minute. First, paste your text, log files, or raw HTML into the input box — it can be messy, long, and full of unrelated content. Second, click "Extract Emails & Links" and the tool scans the whole text with pattern matching, pulling out every email address and website URL it finds. Third, review the results in two tidy, categorised lists: one for unique emails and one for unique links. Finally, copy either list with one click, ready to paste into a spreadsheet, an email client, or a document.

## Feature Deep Dive

The extractor uses pattern matching to recognise the shape of an email address and a URL inside any surrounding text, so it works on log dumps, exported spreadsheets, copied web pages, and raw HTML alike. Duplicates are filtered automatically — if the same address appears fifty times in a log file, it shows up once in the results, which saves a lot of manual cleanup. The two lists are kept separate, so emails and links never get mixed together, and each list can be copied on its own. Because the scanning runs in local JavaScript, even a very long document is processed quickly, and there is no limit imposed by a server. Your pasted text is never stored: close the tab and it is gone.

## Use Cases

Email and link extraction is a quiet workhorse for many jobs. Recruiters pull contact addresses out of piles of CVs and cover letters. Marketers gather addresses from event sign-up sheets and exported forms to build a mailing list. Developers extract URLs from log files and error reports to find broken links, and researchers pull links out of copied articles and reference lists. It is also useful at home: sorting out a messy contact export, recovering addresses from an old email thread you copied into a document, or collecting all the links from a long page of notes into one tidy list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What kinds of text can I paste in?**

Almost anything — plain text, log files, raw HTML, copied web pages, spreadsheets pasted as text, and exported documents. The extractor ignores the surrounding content and picks out only the emails and links.

**Can I extract only emails and ignore the links?**

The results are shown in two separate lists, so you can simply copy the email list and ignore the links list, or the other way around.

**What if the same email appears many times in my text?**

It will appear only once in the results. Duplicates are removed automatically, so the list you copy is already clean.

**Is there a limit on how much text I can paste?**

There is no server limit, because everything runs in your browser. Very large pastes depend on your device's memory, but ordinary documents, logs, and exports are handled easily.
