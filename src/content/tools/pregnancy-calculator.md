## Why Client-Side Matters

A due date calculation involves some of the most personal information you will ever type into a website: the date of your last period and the details of your pregnancy. That information has no business travelling to a server. This tool runs the whole calculation inside your own browser, on your device, and nothing you enter is uploaded, stored, or shared. The result is an estimate to help you plan, not medical advice, and your dates stay private while you use it.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Start by picking the first day of your last menstrual period using the date picker. If your cycle is longer or shorter than average, adjust the cycle length box; the default is 28 days. Then click "Calculate Due Date" and the tool works out your estimated delivery date. Below the result you will see your current pregnancy week, which trimester you are in, and how many days remain until the due date. You can change the date or the cycle length at any time and recalculate to see how the estimate moves.

## Feature Deep Dive

The calculator is built on Naegele's rule, the standard method of estimating a due date by counting 40 weeks, or 280 days, from the first day of the last menstrual period. Adjusting the cycle length matters because the rule assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14; a longer or shorter cycle shifts the estimated conception date and therefore the due date. Alongside the date itself, the tool shows your current week of pregnancy and your trimester, so you can track where you are at a glance, and it counts down the days remaining so you can plan appointments, leave, and preparations around a single clear number.

## Use Cases

Expecting parents use the calculator first to find out roughly when their baby is due, then keep coming back to check which week they are in as the pregnancy progresses. It helps with practical planning: booking antenatal appointments, arranging maternity leave, preparing the nursery, and knowing when to expect the third trimester. Partners and family members use it to follow along and plan support around the due date. It is also useful early on, when a home test is positive but the first scan is still weeks away, giving a rough date to work with until a clinician confirms it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How accurate is the estimated due date?**

It is an estimate, not a prediction. The calculation assumes a regular cycle and a standard 40-week pregnancy, but real pregnancies vary, and many babies arrive days or even weeks either side of the estimated date. Your doctor or midwife can confirm a date with an ultrasound scan.

**What if my cycle is irregular?**

Enter your average cycle length if you know it, but treat the result with extra caution. Irregular cycles make any date-based estimate less reliable, because ovulation does not fall on a predictable day. An early ultrasound gives a much more accurate date in that case.

**Can I use a date from an ultrasound scan instead?**

The calculator works from the first day of your last period. If a scan gives you a different due date, follow the scan date your clinician gives you, since it is based on measurements of the baby rather than on cycle dates.

**Is my information kept private?**

Yes. The whole calculation runs in your browser. Your dates are never uploaded, stored, or shared, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
