## Why Client-Side Matters

An interest calculation involves your money — savings, a loan, or an investment you are considering. Those figures should not travel to a server just to be projected. This tool runs entirely inside your browser, so the amounts, rates, and terms you type never leave your device, are never stored, and vanish when you close the tab. You get a full growth schedule without an account or any financial data leaving your machine.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Choose your mode first: Compound Interest or Simple Interest. Enter the starting principal — the amount you are saving or borrowing — then the annual interest rate and the term in years and months. For compound interest, pick how often interest is compounded: annually, quarterly, monthly, or daily. The results appear at once: the final amount, the total interest earned or owed, the effective annual yield, and a year-by-year growth table showing how the balance changes over time.

## Feature Deep Dive

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal, using the formula interest equals principal times rate times time. Compound interest is different: it earns interest on the interest already earned, following the formula A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where n is how many times per year interest compounds. That is why compounding frequency matters — monthly compounding grows slightly faster than annual compounding at the same rate. The effective annual yield (APY) captures this in a single figure: APY equals (1 + r/n)^n − 1, showing the true yearly return once compounding is counted. The growth table makes the long-term effect visible year by year. Reading down the table, you can see the curve bend upwards as the balance grows — the later years add far more than the early ones, which is the whole reason compound growth rewards patience.

## Use Cases

Savers compare what a deposit would earn at different rates or compounding frequencies before choosing an account. Borrowers see the total interest a loan would cost over its term, not just the monthly payment. Anyone planning for a goal — a house deposit, a car, a holiday — checks whether their savings will reach the target in time. Students learn the difference between simple and compound interest with real numbers in front of them. People comparing two investment offers use the APY figure to judge them fairly, since it accounts for compounding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does compounding frequency change the result?**

Because interest earned earlier starts earning its own interest sooner. Monthly compounding adds interest twelve times a year instead of once, so each addition begins growing right away, and the effect builds up over time.

**Is the projection a promise of what I will earn?**

No. It is a mathematical projection based on the rate you entered. Real savings rates change, investments go up and down, and fees and taxes are not included — so treat the result as an estimate for planning, not a guarantee.

**Does the calculator include tax on the interest?**

No. Tax rules differ by country and by account type, and the tool does not know your tax situation. Subtract any tax you expect to owe from the interest figure yourself to get the after-tax result.

**What is the difference between the interest rate and the APY?**

The interest rate is the nominal yearly rate; the APY is the effective yearly return after compounding is counted. When compounding happens more than once a year, the APY is slightly higher than the nominal rate.
