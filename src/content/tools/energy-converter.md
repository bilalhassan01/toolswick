This tool does every conversion inside your browser, so the numbers you type never travel to a server, are never logged, and disappear when you close the tab. There is nothing to install and no account involved, just a fast, private answer.

## Step-by-Step Guide

First choose what you are converting: Energy (the total capacity to do work) or Power (the rate at which work is done). Type your value into the entry box, then pick the unit you are starting from and the unit you want. The converted result appears immediately, and a copy button puts it on your clipboard. Below the result, a comparison table shows the same value across the other units, so you can see the full set of equivalents without converting one pair at a time.

## Feature Deep Dive

Energy and power are related but different things, and the tool keeps them in separate modes so they are never mixed up. Energy is measured in joules; power is measured in watts, where one watt equals one joule per second. The energy mode covers joules, kilojoules, calories, kilocalories, kilowatt-hours, watt-hours, BTUs, and electronvolts. The power mode covers watts, kilowatts, and mechanical horsepower. One food Calorie — with a capital C — is actually a kilocalorie, equal to 1,000 small calories or 4,184 joules. One kilowatt-hour, the unit on your electricity bill, equals exactly 3,600,000 joules. The power mode covers watts, kilowatts, and mechanical horsepower, where one horsepower is about 745.7 watts. Keeping the two modes separate matters: converting an energy figure directly into watts is a common mistake, and the tool's design prevents it by asking which quantity you mean before showing any units.

## Use Cases

Students convert between joules and calories for physics and chemistry homework, and between watts and horsepower for engineering problems. Homeowners translate the kilowatt-hours on their electricity bill into joules to compare with a solar panel or battery specification. Fitness and nutrition enthusiasts convert the calories on a food label into kilojoules, the unit used on packaging in many countries. Mechanics and engineers compare motor ratings given in horsepower against ratings given in watts. Anyone reading a scientific paper or a foreign datasheet gets the units into the system they actually think in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between energy and power?**

Energy is the total amount of work that can be done; power is how fast that work happens. A battery stores energy, measured in watt-hours, while a motor's power, measured in watts, says how quickly it can use that energy.

**Why do food labels use kilojoules in some countries and calories in others?**

Different regions adopted different systems. Most of the world labels food energy in kilojoules, while the US uses Calories (kilocalories). They measure the same thing — 1 kcal equals about 4.184 kJ.

**What is a BTU?**

A British Thermal Unit is an older energy unit still used for heating and cooling equipment, especially air conditioners. One BTU is roughly 1,055 joules, and air conditioner capacity is often quoted in BTUs per hour.

**Can I convert electronvolts?**

Yes. Electronvolts appear in physics and electronics, and the energy mode includes them alongside joules and calories, with the same instant conversion and comparison table.
