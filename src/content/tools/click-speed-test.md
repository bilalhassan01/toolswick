## Why Client-Side Matters

Clicking speed sounds trivial until you are in a game where a fraction of a second decides the outcome — or until you want to know whether your mouse, your hand, or your technique is holding you back. Many speed tests are wrapped in ads, pop-ups, or pages that track you. This one runs entirely in your browser: a big target, a timer, and your clicks per second, with no account, no tracking, and nothing to install.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Testing your speed takes seconds. First, select a test duration: 1, 5, 10, or 30 seconds. A short test measures raw burst speed; a longer one measures stamina. Second, click the big target button repeatedly as fast as you can. The timer starts on your very first click, so there is no countdown to wait through. Third, keep clicking until the time runs out. Finally, review your final score: your total clicks, your clicks per second (CPS), and your speed ranking, which tells you how your result compares.

## Feature Deep Dive

The test measures clicks per second, the standard number gamers use to compare clicking speed, along with your total clicks over the chosen duration. Normal mouse clicking averages around 6 to 8 CPS, while practiced techniques push much higher — jitter clicking, where the hand vibrates to click faster, and butterfly clicking, where two fingers alternate on the same button, are the two methods competitive players train. The different durations test different things: a 1-second test is pure burst, 5 and 10 seconds show your comfortable pace, and 30 seconds reveals whether your speed drops as your hand tires. The ranking gives you a label for your result — from slow to fast — so you can see progress at a glance, and the "Restart" button resets the counter instantly for another try.

## Use Cases

Click speed tests are a staple of gaming culture. Minecraft PvP players measure their CPS because faster clicking means faster attacks, and players of other competitive games use it to check their mouse and their hand before a match. It is also a fun challenge between friends — who can click fastest in ten seconds — and a way to test a new mouse or a new clicking technique to see if it actually helps. Beyond gaming, it is a simple reaction and stamina check: how fast can your hand move, and how long can it keep that pace up before slowing down. Some people use it as a warm-up before a gaming session, the way an athlete warms up before a match.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What clicking technique gives the highest CPS?**

Butterfly clicking — alternating two fingers on one button — usually gives the highest numbers, followed by jitter clicking. Normal clicking with one finger is slower but steadier and safer for your hand.

**Does my mouse affect my CPS score?**

Yes, to a degree. A mouse with a light, responsive button clicks faster than a stiff one, and a high polling rate helps. But technique and practice matter far more than the hardware.

**Why is my CPS lower in a 30-second test than in a 5-second test?**

Because stamina matters. Most people can burst quickly for a few seconds, then slow down as the hand tires. The longer test measures how well you sustain your speed.

**Can I hurt my hand by clicking too fast?**

Take breaks between attempts and stop if your wrist or fingers hurt. Jitter clicking in particular puts strain on the hand, so practise in short sessions rather than for hours at a time.
