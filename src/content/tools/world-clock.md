## Why Client-Side Matters

Checking the time in another country is a small, everyday task, and it should not require an account or a server call. This tool shows live world clocks and plans meetings across time zones entirely inside your own browser. Nothing you enter is uploaded or stored, and nothing remains behind when you close the tab. You can check a time or plan a call in complete privacy.

## Step-by-Step Guide

The page shows live digital clocks for major cities, including UTC, New York, London, Paris, Dubai, Tokyo, and Sydney, all ticking in real time. To plan a meeting, open the Timezone Meeting Planner, pick a date and time, and choose the base city the time refers to. Every clock on the page instantly shows the matching local time for that meeting, so you can see at a glance who would be joining at midnight and who at midday. Click "Live Syncing" at any time to return the clocks to real-time ticking.

## Feature Deep Dive

The clocks are powered by your browser's built-in time zone engine, which uses the official IANA time zone database, the same source that operating systems and phones rely on. That matters because time zones are not simple offsets: daylight saving time shifts them through the year, and the rules differ by country and sometimes by region. The tool applies those shifts automatically, so a meeting planned for July shows the correct summer offset for London and New York, while the same meeting in January shows the winter offset, with no manual adjustment needed. The planner works in both directions: set a time in your city and see it everywhere else, or set a time in a colleague's city and see what it means for you.

## Use Cases

Remote teams use the planner to find a meeting time that works across continents, avoiding calls that land at 3 in the morning for someone. Travellers check the time at their destination before calling home or booking a transfer. Freelancers use it to schedule calls with clients in other countries, and families spread across the world use it to find a good time for a video call. Anyone following a live event, a broadcast, or a deadline in another time zone uses the clocks to translate the time into their own at a glance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many cities does the world clock show?**

The page shows live clocks for major international cities, including UTC, New York, London, Paris, Dubai, Tokyo, and Sydney. The meeting planner then calculates the matching local time for each of those cities for any date and time you choose.

**What happens when daylight saving time changes?**

The clocks adjust automatically. Because the tool uses the browser's IANA time zone database, it applies each region's daylight saving rules for the date you pick, so a meeting planned before a clock change and one planned after it both show the correct local times.

**Can I plan a meeting for a date months in the future?**

Yes. Pick any date in the planner, and the tool calculates the local times for that date, including the daylight saving rules in force then. That makes it useful for scheduling calls, flights, and events well ahead of time.

**Is anything I type sent anywhere?**

No. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser, using your device's own time zone data. Nothing is uploaded or stored, and closing the tab leaves nothing behind.
