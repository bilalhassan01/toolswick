## Why Client-Side Matters

A fuel estimate involves your trip, your car, and the price you pay at the pump — details of your own travel that do not need to leave your device. This tool runs the whole calculation inside your browser, so the distances, consumption figures, and prices you type are never uploaded, never stored, and vanish when you close the tab. You get a clear trip budget without an account or any data leaving your machine.

## Step-by-Step Guide

First choose your measurement system: Metric (kilometres, litres per 100 km, price per litre) or Imperial (miles, miles per gallon, price per gallon). Enter the total trip distance, your vehicle's fuel consumption, and the current fuel price. If you are travelling with others, enter the number of passengers and the tool divides the cost automatically. The results appear at once: total fuel needed, total cost, cost per passenger, and cost per mile or kilometre. Change any figure and the budget updates instantly.

## Feature Deep Dive

In metric mode, the litres needed equal the distance divided by 100, multiplied by the litres-per-100km figure; in imperial mode, gallons needed equal distance divided by miles per gallon. The tool also converts between the two consumption ratings automatically — US miles per gallon equals 235.215 divided by litres per 100 km — so you can compare a car rated in one system against a car rated in the other. The per-passenger split divides the total evenly, which settles the question of who owes what before the trip starts. Cost per mile or per kilometre is shown too, which is the figure that makes different trips easy to compare. If your route mixes city and motorway driving, try entering your real-world consumption rather than the best-case figure — most cars use more fuel in stop-start traffic than on a steady motorway run.

## Use Cases

Road-trippers budget a holiday drive before leaving, checking whether the fuel cost fits the plan. Friends sharing a car split the fuel bill fairly with the per-passenger figure instead of arguing about it afterwards. Commuters compare the weekly cost of driving against a train or bus fare. Car buyers compare two models' consumption ratings in the same units to see which is genuinely cheaper to run. Delivery drivers and couriers work out the fuel cost of a day's route and what it means per job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How accurate is the fuel estimate?**

It is as accurate as the figures you enter. Real consumption varies with traffic, hills, air conditioning, and driving style, so treat the result as a good estimate rather than a guarantee — and keep a small margin in your budget.

**Should I use the manufacturer's consumption figure or my own?**

Your own, if you know it. Manufacturer figures come from standardised tests, and most cars use more in everyday driving. Track a few fill-ups and use your real average for the most honest estimate.

**Can I include tolls and other trip costs?**

No — this tool covers fuel only. Add tolls, parking, and food separately to get the full trip budget, using the fuel total as the starting point.

**What if fuel prices differ along my route?**

Use the highest price you expect to pay, or an average of the prices along the way. The tool lets you change the price instantly, so you can test both and see the range.
