## Why Client-Side Matters

A long document without page numbers is hard to use. "See page 12" means nothing when the pages are not numbered, and a printed report, thesis, or contract loses its place the moment the pages are shuffled. Most online numbering tools ask you to upload the document to a server you do not control, where a copy can sit in storage or pass through logs. This tool works differently. Your PDF is numbered entirely inside your own browser using the pdf-lib engine, in memory, on your device. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing remains behind after you close the tab.

## Step-by-Step Guide

Adding page numbers here takes less than a minute, and every step happens on the same page. First, drop your PDF into the workspace, or click to browse and select it from your computer or phone. Second, choose your numbering style — "Page X of Y", "Page X", or a single number — and pick where the numbers should sit: top or bottom of the page, aligned left, centre, or right. Then set the starting number, the font size, the edge margin, and the text colour to match your document. Third, click "Add Page Numbers" to stamp every page and download the finished file to your device.

## Feature Deep Dive

The numbering engine is pdf-lib, an open-source library that runs in your browser and draws the numbers directly onto each page. The numbers are drawn cleanly on top of the existing margins, so your content is never touched: the text, images, and layout of the original pages stay exactly as they were, and the numbers sit where a reader expects them.

Three styles cover the common cases. "Page X of Y" is the clearest for shared documents, because the reader always knows how many pages remain; "Page X" is the classic formal choice; and a single number is the quietest option for documents that should look minimal. Six positions are available — top or bottom, each in left, centre, or right alignment — so the numbers can sit in a footer or a header area. The start number can be set to anything, which matters when the file is one chapter of a larger work. There is no server-imposed limit — the only practical limit is your device's browser memory — and your original file is never changed.
## Use Cases

Page numbers are the kind of small detail that makes a document feel finished. Legal agreements and contracts need them so that clauses can be referenced by page in discussion and in court. Theses, dissertations, and manuscripts need them so examiners and reviewers can point to an exact page. Business proposals and reports look more professional with numbered pages, and a numbered meeting pack lets everyone in the room turn to the same page at the same moment instead of counting from the front.

Numbering also saves a document that has already been through the world. A printed report that gets dropped and shuffled can be reassembled in seconds when every page carries its number, and a scanned book chapter that lost its printed numbers becomes navigable again. Starting the numbering at a number other than one covers the common case of a single chapter or section taken from a longer work — the pages keep their true numbers instead of restarting at one and confusing the reader.
## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which numbering style should I pick?**

"Page X of Y" is the clearest for documents you share, because the reader always knows how many pages remain. "Page X" is the classic formal choice, and a single number is the quietest option for documents that should look minimal.

**Can I make the numbers bigger or smaller?**

Yes. The font size control runs from 8 to 36 points, with 11 points as the default. Smaller sizes suit dense documents; larger sizes suit presentations and documents that will be read from a distance.

**Can I change the colour of the numbers?**

Yes. A text colour picker lets you match the numbers to your document — dark grey or black for formal work, or a colour that matches your branding.

**Will the numbers appear when I print the PDF?**

Yes. The numbers are drawn onto the pages themselves, so they print exactly like any other content in the document, in any PDF reader and on any printer.

**What if my pages already have numbers printed on them?**

Then the tool will add a second set of numbers, which can look confusing. This tool is designed for documents that have no printed numbers — check a page first, and only add numbers where they are missing.
